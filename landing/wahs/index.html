Washington Health Summary (WAHS) · UW CIRG & WA DOH Collaboration

Patient-mediated health summaries from real-world records.

People receiving care across multiple clinics, hospitals, and pharmacies often end up with large, fragmented health exports filled with repeated lab panels, duplicate notes, outdated problem-list stages, and conflicting medication lists. The WA Health Summary workstream explores how clinical rules and open-weight AI can condense these exports into a clear, verifiable summary that patients control and clinicians can trust.

How it works

Four steps from a raw health export to a shareable summary

01 · Condense (Non-Destructive)

Condense without mutating clinical content

Multi-year FHIR R4 exports can exceed 18 megabytes and 7,000 entries—far too large for a clinician to scan or a language model to read at once. Our content-invariant structural condenser (`W12`) strips FHIR transport boilerplate and collapses byte-identical Base64 note attachments while keeping active problems, active medications, allergies, immunizations, and intraday lab timestamps verbatim—exposing older overflow history on demand via read-only chart lookup tools (`W7`).

02 · Reconcile (Skill Findings)

Surface duplicates & conflicts as findings

In a multi-clinic health summary, duplicate orders, same-day registry entries, and overlapping disease stages are clinical findings about care fragmentation—never noise to be silently deleted during ingestion. Dedicated chart-hygiene and reconciliation skills inspect the unmutated chart and surface reviewable Finding Cards (with `Source A vs. Source B` provenance and next-visit checklists) for patients and clinicians.

03 · Optional Privacy (Deferred W9)

Opt-in selective disclosure before external sharing

Health records can contain sensitive social, behavioral, or personal findings alongside medical diagnoses. Rather than baking redaction unconditionally into the general chart condenser, sensitive-record classification, patient-selected redaction, and cryptographic `sd-fhir` (`sd-hash`) / SMART Health Links JWE obfuscation are designed as an optional capability in the deferred Secure FHIR Bundle workstream (`W9`).

04 · Ground & verify

Ask questions with linked source records

Every summary statement, conflict alert, and Q&A response cites the exact FHIR resource identifier and clinical date so a patient or clinician can inspect the underlying record. When asked about facts absent from the chart, the assistant explicitly abstains rather than guessing.

What we evaluate & current status

Grounded summaries and questions backed by source records

Every summary statement, conflict alert, and chat answer links directly to the underlying FHIR record identifier and date. When a question asks about a test, allergy, or procedure that is not in the patient’s chart, the system explicitly states that the information is not documented rather than guessing.

Evaluated across 14 FHIR R4 bundles spanning 6 patients (`Set A` natural, `Set B` multi-clinic perturbations, `Set C` uncoded/note-contradiction messy records, and 3 US Core / IPS clinical transition archetypes: Cardiorenal HFrEF & Hyperkalemia, AFib + Mechanical Valve Dual Anticoagulation & Upper GI Bleed, and Geriatric Polypharmacy & Beers Sedatives) and 39 evaluation cases across 3 separated modalities: Deterministic Pipeline Gate (`W4`), Condensation & Retrieval Ablation (`W7`), and Unhinted Live Local Model & KB Team Evaluation (`W8`/`W13`/`W14`). Deferred tracks: Optional Secure FHIR Bundle (`W9`), CIRG 4-VM H200 GPU server benchmarking (`W10`), and partner-supplied WAHS summaries (`W11`).

Background & Standards Foundation

Researched best practices and clinical standards

Our condensation, reconciliation, privacy segmentation, and human-in-the-loop review workflows are grounded in established health interoperability standards, clinical practice guidelines, federal and Washington state privacy frameworks, and human-AI interaction research. See the detailed design & research report for the full technical synthesis.

FHIR & patient summary standards

  • HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) & ISO 27269 → Defines the mandatory clinical safety core (allergies, active medications, and active problems) preserved across all five condensation tiers.
  • HL7 US Core (FHIR R4) & SMART Health Links → Standards for patient-mediated longitudinal record retrieval and cross-organizational sharing used by WA Health Summary.
  • HL7 AI Transparency on FHIR (AI-Provenance) → Separates supporting FHIR source records, AI model involvement, and recorded human review so automated checks are never conflated with clinical sign-off.

Clinical terminology & guideline authorities

  • KDIGO 2024 CKD & ADA Diabetes Standards → Grounds Layer 1 SNOMED CT disease progression rules, detecting mutually exclusive CKD stages and uncoded renal or glycemic progression across serial labs.
  • NLM RxNorm & DailyMed / MedlinePlus → Grounds Layer 2 brand-to-generic ingredient normalization (e.g., Clopidogrel vs. Plavix, Lisinopril vs. Prinivil) and therapeutic class duplication checks.
  • CDC ACIP Immunization Schedules & LOINC → Grounds Layer 3 vaccine interval validation (CVX) and longitudinal consolidation of blood-versus-serum LOINC laboratory panels.

Privacy segmentation & human-AI interaction

  • 42 CFR Part 2 & WA My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373) → Federal and Washington statutes informing Layer 4 pre-condensation redaction of substance-use, behavioral health, and intimate partner violence findings before external sharing.
  • ONC Cures Act Privacy & HL7 FHIR DS4P → Data Segmentation for Privacy (DS4P) principles distinguishing patient self-review (patient_self) from external provider sharing (external_share).
  • Microsoft HAX Toolkit & NN/g Progressive Disclosure → Human-AI interaction guidelines for efficient correction, source explainability, keeping original records immutable, and preserving unresolved differences.

Critical Review, Phase 3 Status & Deferred Tracks

Implementation status, open questions, and next target areas

Our local Phase 3 validation (`W4`–`W8` & `W12`–`W13`) separates non-destructive structural condensation from skill-based conflict findings and evaluates three distinct modalities (`Deterministic Pipeline Gate`, `Condensation & Retrieval Ablation`, and `Live Model & KB Team Evaluation`), while explicitly tracking deferred team and infrastructure workstreams (`W9`–`W11`). See Section 09 of the detailed design & architecture dashboard for the complete technical analysis, saturation audit, model inventory, and experimental design.

Evaluation rigor & realistic data

  • From rule checks to de-saturated live LLM benchmarking (W4 & W13) → Separating deterministic pipeline gates from unscripted live open-weight LLM runs over unhinted charts, scored with HealthBench weighted criteria, VeriFact atomic entailment, fractional UUID recall, and readability grading.
  • Pragmatic messy synthetic (Set C) & real clinical summaries (W6) → Evaluated across 11 bundles (`Set A/B`, `Set C` with 25% stripped codes and note-vs-medication contradictions, plus 2 de-identified MIMIC-IV-on-FHIR / SMART IPS summary fixtures), with partner WAHS summaries queued in `W11`.
  • Patient-centered question taxonomy first (W5) → Centering scenarios on what patients and caregivers actually ask (“Are two doctors prescribing me the same pill?”, “What changed during my hospital stay?”, “What should I ask at my next visit?”) with a secondary Clinician View toggle.

Models, non-destructive condensation & skills

  • Local model screening (W8) & deferred CIRG H200 GPU run (W10) → Local screening from 1.5B–35B GGUF models on 64 GB unified memory, with 4-VM shared NVIDIA H200 MIG server benchmarking (`uwcirg/llm-configs`) queued for `W10` next week.
  • Prompt engineering & orchestrated KB agent teams (W8 & W13) → Comparing single-pass raw prompting, KB-augmented prompting, cross-family label-only review, and 4-agent specialist orchestration over unhinted charts.
  • Condensation tiers & full-chart queryability (W7) → Keeping the compact IPS safety core in prompt context while making the full Tier 0 historical chart queryable on demand via read-only overflow lookup tools (`search_chart_entries`, `get_lab_history`, `read_clinical_note`).
  • Non-destructive condensation vs. skill-based deduplication (W12) → Running condensation in `lossless_structural` mode without mutating clinical content or pre-tagging conflicts, while surfacing duplicates and discrepancies via explicit chart-hygiene and reconciliation skills.

Deferred optional privacy & audit links

  • Deferred (W9): Optional privacy & element-level FHIR obfuscation → Keeping sensitive-record redaction strictly optional outside the general condenser, scoped to the deferred `W9` Secure FHIR Bundle workstream (`DIGI-UW/ips-selective-disclosures` SD-JWT `sd-hash` + SMART Health Links JWE).
  • Phase 2 evaluation audit & plain-English metric guide → Plain-language definitions of all evaluation metrics, exact measurement formulas, and candid disclosure of current prototype caveats.
