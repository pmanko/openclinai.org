Washington Health Summary (WAHS) · UW CIRG & WA DOH Collaboration

Patient-mediated health summaries from real-world records.

People receiving care across multiple clinics, hospitals, and pharmacies often end up with large, fragmented health exports filled with repeated lab panels, duplicate notes, outdated problem-list stages, and conflicting medication lists. The WA Health Summary workstream explores how clinical rules and open-weight AI can condense these exports into a clear, verifiable summary that patients control and clinicians can trust.

How it works

Four steps from a raw health export to a shareable summary

01 · Condense

Clean and condense large records

Multi-year FHIR R4 exports can exceed 18 megabytes and 7,000 entries—far too large for a clinician to scan or a language model to read at once. A progressive 5-tier condenser removes duplicate notes, groups repeated lab readings into longitudinal trends, and fits the record into a predictable size while keeping every allergy, active medication, and active condition intact.

02 · Reconcile

Catch cross-clinic conflicts

When records arrive from separate clinics or registries, the same medication may appear under both brand and generic names, two drugs from the same class may be listed as active simultaneously, or earlier disease stages may remain marked active alongside newer stages. Deterministic clinical checks highlight these discrepancies so patients and clinicians can review them.

03 · Protect privacy

Choose what to include before sharing

Health records frequently contain sensitive social, behavioral, or personal findings (such as domestic violence screening, justice involvement, or housing instability) stored alongside medical diagnoses. Patients can review their complete personal record in a self-view while automatically redacting sensitive entries when preparing a summary to share with an external provider.

04 · Ground & verify

Ask questions with linked source records

Every summary statement, conflict alert, and Q&A response cites the exact FHIR resource identifier and clinical date so a patient or clinician can inspect the underlying record. When asked about facts absent from the chart, the assistant explicitly abstains rather than guessing.

What we evaluate

Grounded summaries and questions backed by source records

Every summary statement, conflict alert, and chat answer links directly to the underlying FHIR record identifier and date. When a question asks about a test, allergy, or procedure that is not in the patient’s chart, the system explicitly states that the information is not documented rather than guessing.

Tested across synthetic patient records representing an 84-year-old adult with complex heart, kidney, and diabetes care, a 74-year-old adult with multi-clinic specialty records, and a 17-year-old adolescent with school and state registry immunizations.

Background & Standards Foundation

Researched best practices and clinical standards

Our condensation, reconciliation, privacy segmentation, and human-in-the-loop review workflows are grounded in established health interoperability standards, clinical practice guidelines, federal and Washington state privacy frameworks, and human-AI interaction research. See the detailed design & research report for the full technical synthesis.

FHIR & patient summary standards

  • HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) & ISO 27269 → Defines the mandatory clinical safety core (allergies, active medications, and active problems) preserved across all five condensation tiers.
  • HL7 US Core (FHIR R4) & SMART Health Links → Standards for patient-mediated longitudinal record retrieval and cross-organizational sharing used by WA Health Summary.
  • HL7 AI Transparency on FHIR (AI-Provenance) → Separates supporting FHIR source records, AI model involvement, and recorded human review so automated checks are never conflated with clinical sign-off.

Clinical terminology & guideline authorities

  • KDIGO 2024 CKD & ADA Diabetes Standards → Grounds Layer 1 SNOMED CT disease progression rules, detecting mutually exclusive CKD stages and uncoded renal or glycemic progression across serial labs.
  • NLM RxNorm & DailyMed / MedlinePlus → Grounds Layer 2 brand-to-generic ingredient normalization (e.g., Clopidogrel vs. Plavix, Lisinopril vs. Prinivil) and therapeutic class duplication checks.
  • CDC ACIP Immunization Schedules & LOINC → Grounds Layer 3 vaccine interval validation (CVX) and longitudinal consolidation of blood-versus-serum LOINC laboratory panels.

Privacy segmentation & human-AI interaction

  • 42 CFR Part 2 & WA My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373) → Federal and Washington statutes informing Layer 4 pre-condensation redaction of substance-use, behavioral health, and intimate partner violence findings before external sharing.
  • ONC Cures Act Privacy & HL7 FHIR DS4P → Data Segmentation for Privacy (DS4P) principles distinguishing patient self-review (patient_self) from external provider sharing (external_share).
  • Microsoft HAX Toolkit & NN/g Progressive Disclosure → Human-AI interaction guidelines for efficient correction, source explainability, keeping original records immutable, and preserving unresolved differences.

Critical Review & Phase 3 Roadmap

Open questions, current limitations, and next target areas

Our Phase 2 prototype and evaluation validate the deterministic pre-LLM foundation (condensing large FHIR bundles and running clinical rule checks), but important research and engineering questions remain before live clinical or patient-facing deployment. See Section 09 of the detailed design & architecture dashboard for the complete technical analysis, model inventory, and experimental design.

Evaluation rigor & realistic data

  • From rule checks to live LLM benchmarking → Phase 2 evaluates deterministic Python condensation and rule templates. Phase 3 pairs live open-weight LLM runs with OpenAI HealthBench weighted physician rubrics, Stanford MedHELM, and VeriFact/FActScore atomic claim entailment.
  • Realistic, messy multi-EHR synthetic & de-identified data → Expanding beyond three clean Synthea records to 8 patient personas with a 6-Layer EHR Messiness Injector (missing codes, OCR/free-text clinical notes, conflicting sig text, C-CDA/EHI imports) and MIMIC-IV-on-FHIR cases.
  • Patient-centered question taxonomy first → Centering scenarios on what patients and caregivers actually ask (“Are two doctors prescribing me the same pill?”, “What changed during my hospital stay?”, “What should I ask at my next visit?”) with clinician insight secondary.

Models, GPU compute & orchestration

  • Shared GPU execution & model selection funnel → Running and screening models from 1B–4B edge/browser tiers up to 8B–35B server models (Gemma 4, MedGemma, Qwen 3, Granite 4.2) on the UW CIRG NVIDIA H200 server (uwcirg/llm-configs).
  • Prompt engineering & orchestrated KB agent teams → Comparing single-pass prompting, cross-family label-only review, and multi-agent specialist orchestration for knowledge-base-grounded medication, lab, and guideline queries.
  • Condensation tiers & full-chart queryability → Keeping the compact Tier 2–4 IPS safety core in prompt context while making the full Tier 0/1 historical chart queryable on demand via scoped MCP FHIR tools and local embeddings.

Cryptographic FHIR privacy

  • Selective disclosure & element-level FHIR obfuscation → Connecting pre-condensation DS4P filtering with UW DIGI’s ips-selective-disclosures (IETF SD-JWT salted sd-hash extensions on FHIR elements/entries) and SMART Health Links JWE encryption.
  • Phase 2 evaluation audit & plain-English metric guide → Plain-language definitions of all evaluation metrics, exact measurement formulas, and candid disclosure of current prototype caveats.
