OpenClinAI / Projects / WA Health Summary (WAHS)

University of Washington & Washington State Department of Health

Your health records in one place, with answers you can check.

WA Health Summary (WAHS) helps people bring together health records from the different clinics, hospitals, and labs they visit. People can review everything in one place, share a clear summary with their care team, and ask questions about their own records in everyday language. Every answer points back to the original record it came from.

WAHS is a research and development project. Every patient shown on these pages is a synthetic or de-identified example, not a real person.

Why this matters

Health records are scattered, and the important connections are easy to miss

Most people get care from more than one place: a primary care clinic, a specialist, an urgent care visit, a hospital stay. Each place keeps its own records. When those records are finally brought together, they can run to hundreds or thousands of entries, with repeated lab results, long clinical notes, and medicine lists that don't always agree.

Each record can be correct on its own while the full picture still goes unnoticed. Seeing that picture usually means reading everything side by side, which is hard for patients, families, and busy clinicians alike.

An example (synthetic patient)

Margaret's medicines

Margaret is in her early eighties and sees several clinicians, including a geriatrics clinic and a sleep clinic. Over time, two different clinics each prescribed her a medicine that causes drowsiness. Her records also show a recent emergency room visit after a fall, and two cholesterol medicines from the same drug family.

None of this is hidden; it's all in her records. But it is spread across different clinics and dates. When Margaret asks WAHS "What medicines am I taking, and is there anything I should ask my doctor about?", it brings these pieces together, explains the possible concern in plain language, suggests questions she could raise with her care team, and links each point to the original record so she or her clinician can check it.

The apps

Two ways to use WA Health Summary

The WAHS web app and the WAHS mobile companion work from the same secure record, so people can switch between them. Both are built on open health data standards, the same ones many health systems already use to share records.

Web app

WAHS web app

A full view of a person's health record in the browser.

  • Bring in records from different providers and see them together
  • Add your own information, such as your health story, goals, and care needs
  • Create a summary of your key health information and share it through a secure link or QR code
  • Ask questions about your record with Ask WAHS
Mobile companion

WAHS mobile companion

A phone-friendly companion for checking in on the go.

  • See a simple overview of your health summary
  • Update your health story and personal notes from your phone
  • Share your summary with a QR code at an appointment
  • Ask WAHS questions and tap any source to see where an answer came from

How Ask WAHS works

What happens when you ask a question

Behind Ask WAHS is the WAHS AI engine, which prepares your record and works with an AI model to write the answer. Every question goes through the same four steps.

  1. It gathers only your records

    WAHS uses your own sign-in to pull together the records you have access to. It never uses a master key, so it can't see anyone else's information.

  2. It organizes the record

    Duplicate notes and technical clutter are removed, and the rest is laid out clearly. If the record is very large, repeated history is summarized, while allergies, current medicines, and active conditions are always kept.

  3. It adds helpful background

    Depending on the question, WAHS can add dates and trends, notes on which medicines belong to the same family, or relevant passages from long clinical notes.

  4. It answers, and shows its sources

    An AI model writes a plain-language answer, and each statement links to the record it came from. If the record doesn't contain the answer, WAHS says so instead of guessing.

What we're working on

Getting the right information to the AI, and checking that it helps

Much of our work goes into two questions. First, how should a person's record be prepared and presented to an AI model so it can find what matters? This is sometimes called context engineering. Second, how should the model and the helper steps around it be coordinated for each question? This is sometimes called model orchestration.

Testing approaches side by side

We compare approaches that range from simple, like giving the AI a cleanly organized record and nothing else, to more involved ones that add background information and pull in relevant passages from long notes. We try them on the same patients and questions, using open AI models we can run on university computing.

Answers are reviewed against the original records for accuracy, completeness, and safety, and we keep the changes that make a real difference. Detailed results are published openly.

Privacy and safety

  • Your records only. WAHS works only with the records the signed-in person is allowed to see.
  • No stored conversations. Questions and answers aren't saved in a database on the server.
  • Sources for every answer. Each statement can be checked against the original record.
  • Not medical advice. WAHS is meant to help people understand and talk about their records. It doesn't replace a conversation with a clinician.
