Washington Health Summary (WAHS) · UW CIRG & WA DOH Collaboration

Patient-mediated health summaries from real-world records.

People receiving care across multiple clinics, hospitals, and pharmacies often end up with large, fragmented health exports filled with repeated lab panels, duplicate notes, outdated problem-list stages, and conflicting medication lists. The WA Health Summary workstream explores how clinical rules and open-weight AI can condense these exports into a clear, verifiable summary that patients control and clinicians can trust.

How it works

Four steps from a raw health export to a shareable summary

01 · Condense (Non-Destructive)

Condense without mutating clinical content

Multi-year FHIR R4 exports can exceed 18 megabytes and 7,000 entries—far too large for a clinician to scan or a standard language model window to process efficiently. Our content-invariant structural condenser strips FHIR transport boilerplate and collapses byte-identical Base64 note attachments across nine token budgets (`256k` down to `1k`) in two modes (`Lossless Verbatim` and `Grouped Renewal-Folded`). It prioritizes the IPS clinical safety core (allergies, active medications, active problems, and implants) first, folds historical renewals and lab series, and indexes any budget overflow in `[2] Completeness Manifest` for on-demand `Tier 0` chart lookup.

02 · Reconcile (Skill Findings)

Surface duplicates & conflicts as findings

In a multi-clinic health summary, duplicate orders, same-day registry entries, and overlapping disease stages are clinical findings about care fragmentation—never noise to be silently deleted during ingestion. Dedicated chart-hygiene and reconciliation skills inspect the unmutated chart and surface reviewable Finding Cards (with `Source A vs. Source B` provenance and next-visit checklists) for patients and clinicians.

03 · Optional Privacy Segmentation

Opt-in selective disclosure before external sharing

Health records can contain sensitive social, behavioral, or personal-safety findings alongside medical diagnoses. Rather than baking redaction unconditionally into the general chart condenser, patients can toggle interactive external-share privacy segmentation (`42 CFR Part 2` and `WA My Health My Data Act`) in Step 2 to withhold sensitive entries from the condensed chart and AI prompt while logging the redaction count in `[Privacy Manifest]`.

04 · Ground & verify

Ask questions with linked source records

Every summary statement, conflict alert, and Q&A response cites the exact FHIR resource identifier and clinical date so a patient or clinician can inspect the underlying record. When asked about facts absent from the chart, the assistant explicitly abstains rather than guessing.

What we evaluate & current status

Grounded summaries and questions backed by source records

Every summary statement, conflict alert, and chat answer links directly to the underlying FHIR record identifier and date. When a question asks about a test, allergy, or procedure that is not in the patient’s chart, the system explicitly states that the information is not documented rather than guessing.

Evaluated across 18 FHIR R4 bundles spanning 10 patients (3 EHIgnite/Synthea multi-clinic patients across `Set A` natural, `Set B` multi-clinic perturbations, and `Set C` uncoded/note-contradiction messy records; 5 US Core / IPS longitudinal clinical archetypes; and 2 PhysioNet MIMIC-IV-FHIR Demo 2.0 hospital/ICU patients) across three separated evaluation modalities: Deterministic Pipeline Gate, Condensation & Retrieval Ablation (`256k` down to `1k`), and Unhinted Live Model & Knowledge-Base Evaluation.

Background & Standards Foundation

Researched best practices and clinical standards

Our condensation, reconciliation, privacy segmentation, and human-in-the-loop review workflows are grounded in established health interoperability standards, clinical practice guidelines, federal and Washington state privacy frameworks, and human-AI interaction research. See the detailed design & research report for the full technical synthesis.

FHIR & patient summary standards

  • HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) & ISO 27269 → Defines the priority clinical safety core (allergies, active medications, active problems, and implants) prioritized across all nine token budgets (`256k` down to `1k`), with any micro-budget overflow indexed in `[2] Completeness Manifest`.
  • HL7 US Core (FHIR R4) & SMART Health Links → Standards for patient-mediated longitudinal record retrieval and cross-organizational sharing used by WA Health Summary.
  • HL7 AI Transparency on FHIR (AI-Provenance) → Separates supporting FHIR source records, AI model involvement, and recorded human review so automated checks are never conflated with clinical sign-off.

Clinical terminology & guideline authorities

  • KDIGO 2024 CKD & ADA Diabetes Standards → Grounds Layer 1 SNOMED CT disease progression rules, detecting mutually exclusive CKD stages and uncoded renal or glycemic progression across serial labs.
  • NLM RxNorm & DailyMed / MedlinePlus → Grounds Layer 2 brand-to-generic ingredient normalization (e.g., Clopidogrel vs. Plavix, Lisinopril vs. Prinivil) and therapeutic class duplication checks.
  • CDC ACIP Immunization Schedules & LOINC → Grounds Layer 3 vaccine interval validation (CVX) and longitudinal consolidation of blood-versus-serum LOINC laboratory panels.

Privacy segmentation & human-AI interaction

  • 42 CFR Part 2 & WA My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373) → Federal and Washington statutes informing Layer 4 pre-condensation redaction of substance-use, behavioral health, and intimate partner violence findings before external sharing.
  • ONC Cures Act Privacy & HL7 FHIR DS4P → Data Segmentation for Privacy (DS4P) principles distinguishing patient self-review (patient_self) from external provider sharing (external_share).
  • Microsoft HAX Toolkit & NN/g Progressive Disclosure → Human-AI interaction guidelines for efficient correction, source explainability, keeping original records immutable, and preserving unresolved differences.

Critical Review, Phase 3 Status & Research Directions

Implementation status, open questions, and next target areas

Our Phase 3 validation separates non-destructive structural condensation from skill-based conflict findings and evaluates three distinct modalities (`Deterministic Pipeline Gate`, `Condensation & Retrieval Ablation`, and `Live Model & KB Team Evaluation`). See Section 09 of the detailed design & architecture dashboard for the complete technical analysis, saturation audit, model inventory, and experimental design.

Evaluation rigor & realistic data

  • From rule checks to de-saturated live LLM benchmarking → Separating deterministic pipeline gates from unscripted live open-weight LLM runs over unhinted charts, scored with HealthBench weighted criteria, VeriFact atomic entailment, fractional UUID recall, and readability grading.
  • Pragmatic messy synthetic (Set C) & hospital/ICU FHIR cohorts → Evaluated across 18 bundles spanning 10 patients (`Set A/B`, `Set C` with 25% stripped codes and note-vs-medication contradictions, 5 US Core / IPS care-transition archetypes, plus 2 PhysioNet MIMIC-IV-FHIR Demo 2.0 hospital/ICU patients).
  • Patient-centered question taxonomy first → Centering scenarios on what patients and caregivers actually ask (“Are two doctors prescribing me the same pill?”, “What changed during my hospital stay?”, “What should I ask at my next visit?”) with a secondary Clinician View toggle.

Models, non-destructive condensation & skills

  • Local & UW GPU server model families → Comparing 8 open-weight model families from 1.5B–35B (`Gemma 4 12B/26B/31B`, `MedGemma 27B`, `Qwen 3 14B/35B`, `Mistral Nemo 12B`, `Llama 3.1 8B`, `Granite`, `LFM2`) across local and shared UW GPU infrastructure.
  • Knowledge-base toggle & orchestrated KB agent teams → Comparing `📄 Raw Chart Only` (unhinted control) against `🧠 + 4-Layer Clinical KB` (pre-condensation audit findings + clinical entity subgraph) and cross-family specialist verification across any selected model.
  • Research brief: Condensed summaries, targeted lookup, and meaning-based search → Plain-language guide and literature review comparing whole-summary condensation (`94.0/100`), on-demand `Tier 0` overflow lookup (`92.6/100`), and meaning-based search for deep note archives and everyday patient phrasing.
  • Non-destructive condensation vs. skill-based deduplication → Running condensation in `Lossless Verbatim` or `Grouped Renewal-Folded` mode without mutating clinical content or pre-tagging conflicts, while surfacing duplicates and discrepancies via explicit chart-hygiene and reconciliation skills.

Privacy segmentation & audit links

  • Interactive external-share privacy & element-level FHIR obfuscation → Interactive Step 2 external-share privacy segmentation (`42 CFR Part 2` / `WA My Health My Data Act`) with `[Privacy Manifest]` auditing, plus optional cryptographic `DIGI-UW/ips-selective-disclosures` SD-JWT `sd-hash` and SMART Health Links JWE transport.
  • Phase 2 evaluation audit & plain-English metric guide → Plain-language definitions of all evaluation metrics, exact measurement formulas, and candid disclosure of current prototype caveats.
