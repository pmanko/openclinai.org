Washington Health Summary (WAHS) · UW CIRG & WA DOH Collaboration

Patient-mediated health summaries from real-world records.

People receiving care across multiple clinics, hospitals, and pharmacies often end up with large, fragmented health exports filled with repeated lab panels, duplicate notes, outdated problem-list stages, and conflicting medication lists. The WA Health Summary workstream explores how clinical rules and open-weight AI can condense these exports into a clear, verifiable summary that patients control and clinicians can trust.

How it works

Four steps from a raw health export to a shareable summary

01 · Condense

Clean and condense large records

Multi-year FHIR R4 exports can exceed 18 megabytes and 7,000 entries—far too large for a clinician to scan or a language model to read at once. A progressive 5-tier condenser removes duplicate notes, groups repeated lab readings into longitudinal trends, and fits the record into a predictable size while keeping every allergy, active medication, and active condition intact.

02 · Reconcile

Catch cross-clinic conflicts

When records arrive from separate clinics or registries, the same medication may appear under both brand and generic names, two drugs from the same class may be listed as active simultaneously, or earlier disease stages may remain marked active alongside newer stages. Deterministic clinical checks highlight these discrepancies so patients and clinicians can review them.

03 · Protect privacy

Choose what to include before sharing

Health records frequently contain sensitive social, behavioral, or personal findings (such as domestic violence screening, justice involvement, or housing instability) stored alongside medical diagnoses. Patients can review their complete personal record in a self-view while automatically redacting sensitive entries when preparing a summary to share with an external provider.

What we evaluate

Grounded summaries and questions backed by source records

Every summary statement, conflict alert, and chat answer links directly to the underlying FHIR record identifier and date. When a question asks about a test, allergy, or procedure that is not in the patient’s chart, the system explicitly states that the information is not documented rather than guessing.

Tested across synthetic patient records representing an 84-year-old adult with complex heart, kidney, and diabetes care, a 74-year-old adult with multi-clinic specialty records, and a 17-year-old adolescent with school and state registry immunizations.
