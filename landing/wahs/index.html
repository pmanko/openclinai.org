OpenClinAI / Projects / WA Health Summary (WAHS)

Exploratory Applied Research · University of Washington

Patient-controlled health summaries and question answering.

WA Health Summary (WAHS) is a patient-controlled health summary platform developed at the University of Washington Clinical Informatics Research Group. This exploratory project studies how patients can bring together clinical records from separate clinics, hospitals, and laboratories, share a summary with their care team, and ask questions about their medical history with each statement tied back to its source record.

Project status: WAHS is an active research and development prototype. All patient records shown across this site and in recorded demonstrations are synthetic or de-identified examples.

Clinical Context

Fragmented records make clinical connections hard to see

People managing ongoing health conditions often receive care across several organizations: primary care clinics, specialty practices, urgent care centers, independent laboratories, and hospitals. Each organization keeps its own documentation. When a patient retrieves and combines those records, the resulting file can span hundreds or thousands of entries with repeated lab panels, long clinical notes, and medication lists that do not match across sites.

A prescription or lab result from one clinic may look fine on its own, yet conflict with something recorded elsewhere. Spotting those issues usually requires reading separate notes and timelines side by side, which is difficult for patients, caregivers, and clinicians during short appointments.

Example Case · Synthetic Patient

Reviewing Margaret's medications across clinics

Margaret is in her early eighties and sees several clinicians, including a geriatrics clinic and a sleep medicine clinic. Over time, two clinics independently prescribed sedating medications, while another clinic continued two cholesterol medications from the same drug class. Her chart also documents a recent emergency department visit after a fall.

All of these details are in her combined record, spread across separate clinic exports and dates. When Margaret asks WAHS, "What active medications am I taking, and are there any fall or sedation risks I should ask my doctor about?", it pulls the relevant entries together. The response flags the overlapping prescriptions alongside her recent fall, suggests questions for her next visit, and links each point to the source record and date.

User-Facing Applications

The WAHS web app and mobile companion

WA Health Summary provides two interfaces connected to the patient's underlying health record: a desktop web app for detailed review and a mobile companion for phone access. Both interfaces use HL7 FHIR and International Patient Summary standards for health records, so updates made in one appear right away in the other.

Web App

WAHS web app

A workspace for organizing health records and preparing clinical summaries.

  • Import records from different clinics and view conditions, medications, allergies, immunizations, and labs together
  • Add personal notes, including health history, care goals, and care preferences
  • Build an International Patient Summary and share it with clinicians or family members by secure link or QR code
  • Ask questions across the combined record using Ask WAHS and inspect the full record behind any cited point
Mobile Companion

WAHS mobile companion

A mobile view for quick reference and clinic visits.

  • Check a summary of active conditions, medications, allergies, and recent updates
  • Add or update personal notes from a phone
  • Share a QR health summary during clinic, urgent care, or emergency visits
  • Use Ask WAHS to prepare for appointments and tap any numbered citation to inspect the source record

Request Flow

What happens when a question is asked

When a user submits a question in either app, the WAHS AI engine prepares the record and sends it to an open-weight language model in four steps.

  1. Authorized record retrieval

    The engine uses the signed-in user's own session credentials rather than administrative database keys. It gathers records imported from external clinics, hospitals, and labs along with the patient's own notes and care goals. Timestamps are checked on each request so newly added entries are included right away.

  2. Cleaning and prioritizing the record

    Combined exports often include duplicate notes, invalidated entries, and formatting clutter. Before the model sees the chart, the engine removes duplicates and orders entries by date. Smaller records stay intact. For large multi-year histories, repeated routine measurements are condensed while allergies, active medications, active conditions, and implanted devices are kept in full. Any omitted history is logged explicitly.

  3. Adding question-relevant context

    Optional modules can attach supporting context alongside the chart without changing the original records. Depending on the question, they add lab trends, group medications by active ingredient or drug class, pull relevant passages from long hospital notes, or include public clinical guideline excerpts.

  4. Grounded answers and clear limits

    An open-weight language model writes the response and attaches a numbered citation to each clinical claim. Selecting a citation opens the underlying medication order, lab result, or note along with its date and clinic. If the chart does not contain the requested information, the model says so instead of guessing.

Research & Engineering Focus

Context engineering, model orchestration, infrastructure, and guardrails

Working with real clinical records is harder than passing a file to a chat model. Exports vary in size and structure, models miss details inside noisy records, and health tools need strict privacy and citation rules. Our work focuses on four areas.

01 · Context Engineering

Structuring the clinical record

We test how clinical records should be cleaned, sized, and organized before a model reads them. In WAHS, this means finding how much history a model handles well at once and keeping allergies, active medications, and active conditions intact when large charts are condensed. We also measure whether added context, such as lab trends, drug-class groupings, or note passages, helps or adds noise.

02 · Model Orchestration

Coordinating preparation, retrieval, and generation

WAHS separates record preparation and retrieval from answer generation. Deterministic steps organize the chart and pull relevant passages from long or condensed notes before a single model call produces the response. Because each module can be toggled independently, the same service supports both minimal baselines and richer pipelines.

03 · Serving Infrastructure

Open-weight hosting and portability

Both apps stay lightweight by calling a shared, stateless service for record preparation and model routing. We run open-weight models on university GPU servers and local workstations, keeping the prepared chart stable across follow-up turns so replies stream quickly. Standard interfaces let partners change models or hosting setups without modifying the web or mobile apps.

04 · Guardrails & Governance

Privacy, citations, and clinical limits

Privacy and safety controls are enforced in code, not just in prompts. Every request is restricted to the signed-in user's authorized records, and no conversation database is kept on the server. Citations must map to real entries in the chart, missing information is stated plainly instead of guessed, and responses are designed to support discussions with a clinician rather than give medical advice.

Comparing simple and complex approaches

Because each piece of WAHS is modular, we test simple and complex setups side by side on the same multi-clinic and hospital cases. Our evaluation harness compares raw records, condensed records alone, and combinations of timeline, drug-class, note-retrieval, and guideline modules across different open-weight models.

Answers are checked against the source records for accuracy, completeness, citation quality, speed, and safety so design choices follow measured results.
