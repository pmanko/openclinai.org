Washington Health Summary (WAHS) · Design & Rationale

How the WAHS engine works and why its architecture stays focused.

The WAHS Engine is a lean, standalone middleware service sitting between the WA Health Summary applications, the patient's FHIR R4 record store, and an open-weight language model. Its core pipeline focuses on deterministic, domain-neutral record preparation and source provenance: continuous 3-tier verbatim-first condensation paired with stateless OpenAI-compatible routing and dynamic per-turn context sizing, while optional retrieval and clinical-rule enrichers stay modular and patient-facing selective-disclosure policies remain in a separate standards-based sharing layer.

Implementation: wahs-engine on GitHub ↗ · Technical specification: Engine Reference → · Evidence: Published WAHS Benchmarks ↗

Architectural highlights

  1. Domain-neutral condensation core and modular research extensions. Continuous 3-tier verbatim-first condensation keeps the full record intact whenever it fits the active turn budget, applying uniform series folding and HL7 IPS safety-core packing only when constrained. Optional research extensions (Okapi BM25 note search, omitted-record lookup, longitudinal trend math, and clinical reconciliation) sit behind a clean extension boundary disabled in the baseline runtime.
  2. Stateless runtime & dynamic per-turn sizing. The runtime forwards the caller's token through the fhir-auth security proxy, measures exact conversation and response overhead against the model's context window on every turn (supporting automatic window utilization, calibrated Auto targets, or explicit 2k–64k test budgets), and serves clean OpenAI-compatible models (wahs-26b default, wahs-12b, wahs-e4b).
  3. Grounded in 1,800 H200 benchmark runs & open standards. Large-scale H200 evaluations showed structured condensation paired with open-weight Gemma 4 models substantially outperforms raw uncondensed FHIR dumps and brittle multi-step tool loops while preserving inline [N] provenance to source records.

High-level architecture

How a patient question moves through the system

Three core components do the work in a straight line: the patient app handles sign-in and displays citations, the WAHS Engine retrieves scoped records and runs 3-tier verbatim-first condensation, and a stateless model server writes the cited reply. Optional research modules live behind an extension boundary disabled by default.

Explicit scope & privacy boundary: wahs-engine owns stateless per-turn FHIR bundle retrieval via fhir-auth, continuous 3-tier verbatim-first structural condensation, optional modular research extensions, dynamic per-turn context sizing, and x_wahs citation mapping. Patient selective-disclosure and redaction policies are intentionally kept outside this core runtime so clinical records are never altered or omitted implicitly without standards-based sharing controls. The engine also does not own OIDC login UI, persistent conversation databases, HAPI admin credentials, SMART Health Link cryptography, GPU VRAM scheduling, or evaluation judging (Validation Harness).

Research, standards & best-practice foundations

Why each component exists, and the sources behind it

Each stage of the system and its evaluation harness implements peer-reviewed findings from long-context language model research, healthcare interoperability specifications, clinical guidelines, and privacy law.

Step 1 · Retrieve & Verify Freshness

Patient-mediated FHIR APIs and version-stamped caching

Patients aggregate records across clinics through Cures Act FHIR R4 APIs. Checking lightweight meta.lastUpdated version stamps before pulling full bundles keeps charts current in ~10 ms when nothing changed.

  • HL7 US Core (FHIR R4) ↗ & ONC Cures Act Final Rule ↗ · Standard patient-access clinical profiles and longitudinal record retrieval.
  • SMART Health Links ↗ & HL7 FHIR DS4P Security Labels ↗ · Patient-mediated sharing and per-user access scoping via uwcirg/jwt-proxy ↗.
  • HL7 FHIR RESTful Versioning (meta.lastUpdated) ↗ · Optimistic cache revalidation across multi-clinic datasets (session & scope design →).
Step 2 · Verbatim-First Condensation

Long-context attention limits and clinical safety priority

Language models lose recall when key facts sit buried in verbose multi-megabyte JSON exports. Keeping records verbatim whenever capacity allows, and otherwise prioritizing the international clinical safety core while logging exact omissions, prevents silent truncation and fabrication.

  • Liu et al., Lost in the Middle (TACL, 2024) ↗ · Demonstrates U-shaped long-context recall degradation, motivating concise clinical context over raw 200k+ FHIR JSON dumps.
  • HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) & ISO 27269 ↗ · Defines the mandatory safety core (allergies, active meds, active problems, implants) preserved first under tight budgets.
  • Robertson & Zaragoza, Okapi BM25 (2009) ↗ · Grounds exact clinical-term passage retrieval when long hospital notes exceed budget in opt-in extension evaluations.
Modular Extensions & Privacy Boundary

Isolated R&D enrichers & standards-based disclosure scope

Domain-specific clinical reconciliation and retrieval helpers sit cleanly behind a modular extension interface (disabled by default in baseline runs), while patient selective-disclosure rules remain scoped to dedicated consent and DS4P workflows.

  • HL7 Clinical Information Reconciliation (CIRI) ↗ & WHO SMART Guidelines ↗ · Experimental clinical graph and guideline rules evaluated alongside condensation in Med Agent Hub →.
  • 42 CFR Part 2 ↗ & WA My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373) ↗ · Federal and Washington privacy frameworks guiding future standards-based selective disclosure.
Step 3 · Stateless Prompt & Citations

Prefix KV-cache reuse, inline citations, and human review

Placing the stable chart ledger first lets GPU inference servers reuse cached attention states across follow-up turns, while inline [1]..[N] source tags make every clinical statement inspectable against its original record.

  • Kwon et al., PagedAttention / vLLM (SOSP, 2023) ↗ & Zheng et al., SGLang / RadixAttention (NeurIPS, 2024) ↗ · Prefix KV-cache reuse motivating the invariant chart prefix / turn-local suffix split.
  • Gao et al., ALCE: Generating Text with Citations (EMNLP, 2023) ↗ & PhysioNet ArchEHR-QA (BioNLP 2025) ↗ · Inline bracketed [N] evidence grounding over longitudinal EHR records.
  • HL7 AI Transparency on FHIR (AI-Provenance) ↗ & Amershi et al. (CHI, 2019) / Microsoft HAX ↗ · Progressive source disclosure and explicit human-review signaling.
Cross-Cutting · Blinded Evaluation & Claim Entailment

Why we verify outside the model with physician rubrics, atomic entailment, and two-judge consensus

Clinical AI evaluations show that LLM self-verification accepts incorrect medical answers over 60% of the time (Jin et al., 2026 ↗). Instead of asking the answering model to grade itself, the OpenClinAI Validation Harness → combines deterministic budget and citation checks with atomic EHR fact verification and two independent blinded judges:

  • HealthBench (OpenAI, 2025) ↗ & Stanford MedHELM ↗ · Multi-axis rubric scoring with strict-majority clinical harm consensus across published WAHS benchmark packets ↗.
  • Chung et al., VeriFact (2025) ↗ & Tang et al., MiniCheck (EMNLP, 2024) ↗ · Atomic claim-to-EHR entailment checking so cited [N] records genuinely support stated values, dates, and statuses.
  • Angelopoulos et al., Prediction-Powered Inference (Science, 2023) ↗ · Statistical framework for calibrating automated judge scores against clinician adjudication, paired with interactive exploration in the WAHS Explorer Sandbox ↗.

Methods & mechanisms

Core condensation guarantees and runtime behavior

The standalone engine ↗ pairs a pure-standard-library FHIR R4 preparation and condensation core with a stateless OpenAI-compatible runtime that handles caller authentication, version-stamped caching, exact turn budgeting, and streamed citation mapping.

1. Continuous verbatim-first condensation & modular extensions

Core Mechanism

3-tier verbatim-first condensation

  • Verbatim when it fits (Tier 0): When unbordered or within available turn tokens, emits the entire extracted clinical record and all decoded notes verbatim.
  • Uniform folding & packing (Tier 1 / 2): Only over budget, uniformly folds repeated identical series by FHIR code and unit, anchors the HL7 IPS safety core, and dynamically water-fills clinical notes across all remaining tokens.
Modular Boundary

Opt-in research extensions

  • Clean baseline isolation: Baseline prompt construction passes caller questions verbatim without loading retrieval or guideline modules.
  • Selectable research plug-ins: Operators can enable Okapi BM25 note search, omitted-record lookup, longitudinal trend summaries, or clinical reconciliation behind a uniform extension interface.
Clinical Fidelity

FHIR R4 status & privacy scope

  • Invalidated status hygiene: Excludes entered-in-error and not-done entries, demotes refuted allergies, and deduplicates exact duplicate notes while preserving RxNorm, SNOMED CT, LOINC, and CVX codings.
  • Explicit sharing boundary: Patient-controlled disclosure policies belong in dedicated SMART Health Link and DS4P workflows rather than implicit lexical heuristics.

2. Per-patient caching, dynamic sizing & live streaming

Freshness & Tenant Isolation

Live verification & exact tokenizers

  • Checks fhir-auth every turn: Validates linked patient record modification stamps before reading memory-bounded caches, supports optional local RS256 JWKS defense-in-depth, and isolates caches strictly per user identity and record scope.
  • Exact offline sizing: Uses bundled model tokenizers to measure conversation and response overhead directly against the model's context window, ensuring prompts fit without silent truncation.
Single-Turn Clean Runtime

Preflight guarantees & OpenAI parity

  • Preflight status guarantees: Validates prompt capacity and establishes the upstream inference stream before committing HTTP 200 SSE headers, stripping unclosed reasoning tags cleanly on length limits.
  • Portable OpenAI contract: Forwards standard sampling and streaming options, offloads CPU-bound condensation off the async event loop, and returns inline [N] source provenance plus HL7 AI-Provenance metadata.

Design principles

Five architectural principles guiding the engine

Every design choice in wahs-engine ↗ optimizes for clinical fidelity, predictable behavior under tight context limits, strict tenant isolation, and transparent external evaluation.

  1. Separate condensation mechanism from context-sizing policy.

    The condensation core has no hardcoded token cap or static section quotas: it emits records verbatim whenever capacity allows and otherwise compresses uniformly, while the runtime measures each turn's real prompt and output overhead directly against the model's context window (supporting calibrated Auto sweet-spot targets or explicit 2k–64k test presets).

  2. Never grant the AI middleware elevated record-store privileges.

    Because the engine holds zero FHIR administrative credentials and forwards only the caller's own signed bearer token through fhir-auth, per-user authorization and tenant boundaries are enforced before any health record enters memory or reaches an inference server.

  3. Preserve clinical meaning without silent deletion or mutation.

    Invalidated statuses (such as entered-in-error records) are excluded cleanly, multi-source conflicting entries remain intact rather than silently merged, and any items trimmed under tight budgets are explicitly counted in the top-level Completeness Manifest.

  4. Keep research enrichers and selective-disclosure privacy modular.

    BM25 note search, omitted-record lookup, longitudinal trend math, and clinical reconciliation attach only through an explicit extension interface, while patient-controlled privacy and redaction remain decoupled for dedicated HL7 FHIR DS4P ↗ and SMART Health Link sharing workflows.

  5. Bind every answer to inspectable sources and evaluate externally.

    Stateless OpenAI-compatible streaming attaches numbered [N] FHIR resource citations and HL7 AI-Provenance metadata on every turn, backed by independent blinded multi-judge benchmarks on reports.openclinai.org ↗.
