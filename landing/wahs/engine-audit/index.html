Washington Health Summary (WAHS) · Verified Baseline Audit

How the WAHS engine works and why its baseline is verified.

The WAHS Engine is a lean, standalone service that sits between the WA Health Summary app, the patient's health record store, and an open-weight language model. This audit documents the engine's exact scope and non-goals, links the clinical and privacy foundations behind each stage, summarizes 1,800 H200 benchmark runs, and records the verified resolution of all 27 architectural and clinical audit items.

Verified 25 November 2026 · wahs-engine on GitHub ↗ (main at 808d16a via PR #10 & PR #12) · Automated suites: 96 / 96 tests passing (94 offline + 2 live HAPI/Keycloak)

Verified baseline summary

  1. Clean scope and pure-stdlib clinical core. wahs_core (100% Python standard library, verified under python -S) handles 90–99% FHIR R4 condensation, 7-edge clinical reconciliation, longitudinal lab trends, and sharing redaction; wahs_engine adds stateless OpenAI routing, fhir-auth bundle assembly, optional RS256 JWKS verification, and exact offline token sizing.
  2. Condensing first beats iterative AI tool-calling on both quality and safety. Across 900 graded runs on UW CIRG's NVIDIA H200, Gemma 4 26B with a 16k condensed chart + 4-Layer KB (wahs-26b-kb) scored 85.1 / 100 with zero confirmed unsafe answers (3.4 s median wait), whereas forcing models to search a 2k summary via tool calls dropped scores to 65.8–69.4 and produced two-thirds of all unsafe answers.
  3. All 27 audit items (E1–E11, C1–C10) and companion PRs are merged and tested on main. Runtime single-flight pooling, budget caps, streaming preflight status codes, unclosed <think> stripping, invalidated FHIR status filters, electrolyte salt preservation, and defense-in-depth sensitivity rules are all regression-tested on main without bundling out-of-scope behavioral guardrails.

High-level architecture

How a patient question moves through the system

Three services share the work so each stays simple and replaceable: the patient app handles sign-in and conversation UI, the WAHS Engine prepares the patient's record and clinical checks, and a stateless model server writes the plain-language answer.

Explicit scope & architectural non-goals: wahs-engine owns stateless per-turn FHIR bundle assembly, deterministic condensation, pre-generation clinical reconciliation, fail-closed sharing_posture filtering, exact offline token budgeting, and x_wahs source-citation mapping. It intentionally does not own OIDC login UI, persistent conversation databases, HAPI admin credentials, SMART Health Link cryptography (shl-server), GPU multi-model residency scheduling, or automated evaluation judging (validation-harness). For 42 CFR Part 2 and WA MHMDA external shares, deterministic code-and-lexical redaction inside wahs_core serves as defense-in-depth alongside upstream HAPI meta.security tagging.

Research, standards & best-practice foundations

Why each step in the engine exists, and the sources behind it

Each of the four steps in the diagram above (plus our evaluation harness) implements established findings from long-context LLM research, healthcare interoperability standards, clinical practice guidelines, and privacy law.

Step 1 · Fetch & Check Freshness

Patient-mediated FHIR APIs and version-stamped caching

Patients gather records across clinics through Cures Act FHIR R4 APIs. Checking lightweight meta.lastUpdated version stamps before pulling full bundles keeps charts fresh in ~10 ms when nothing changed.

  • HL7 US Core (FHIR R4) ↗ & ONC Cures Act Final Rule ↗ · Standard patient-access clinical profiles and Patient/$everything retrieval.
  • SMART Health Links ↗ & HL7 FHIR DS4P Security Labels ↗ · Patient-mediated sharing and meta.security access scoping via uwcirg/jwt-proxy ↗.
  • HL7 FHIR RESTful Versioning (meta.lastUpdated) ↗ · Optimistic cache revalidation across multi-clinic datasets (session & scope design →).
Step 2 · Condense Record (90–99%)

Long-context attention limits and clinical safety priority

Language models lose recall when key facts sit buried in noisy multi-megabyte JSON. Pinning an international clinical safety core first and explicitly logging omitted counts prevents silent truncation and guessing.

  • Liu et al., Lost in the Middle (TACL, 2024) ↗ · Demonstrates U-shaped long-context recall degradation, motivating compact 16k condensation over raw 200k+ FHIR dumps.
  • HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) & ISO 27269 ↗ · Defines the mandatory safety core (allergies, active meds, active problems, implants) preserved first across all budgets.
  • Robertson & Zaragoza, Okapi BM25 (2009) ↗ · Grounds exact clinical-term passage retrieval (note_rag) when long hospital notes exceed budget.
Step 3 · Clinical & Privacy Rules

Symbolic clinical reconciliation and data-layer redaction

Cross-clinic discrepancies (such as an urgent-care note holding an outpatient pill) are safety signals to surface, and sensitive-record exclusions belong in deterministic code before prompt assembly.

  • HL7 Clinical Information Reconciliation (CIRI) ↗ & WHO SMART Guidelines ↗ · Computable clinical reconciliation ahead of prose generation (Med Agent Hub →).
  • KDIGO 2024 CKD ↗, ADA Diabetes ↗, NLM RxNorm ↗, LOINC ↗ / UCUM ↗ & CDC ACIP ↗ · Renal/potassium cutoffs, active-ingredient matching, unit conversion, and vaccine intervals.
  • 42 CFR Part 2 ↗ & WA My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373) ↗ · Statutory basis for data-layer external_share redaction and scope-isolated chat sessions.
Step 4 · Split Prompt & Attach Links

Prefix KV-cache reuse, inline citations, and human review

Keeping messages[0] identical across questions lets GPU servers reuse cached attention blocks (cutting follow-ups from ~19s to ~3s), while [1]..[N] source links make every claim inspectable.

  • Kwon et al., PagedAttention / vLLM (SOSP, 2023) ↗ & Zheng et al., SGLang / RadixAttention (NeurIPS, 2024) ↗ · Prefix KV-cache reuse motivating the invariant messages[0] / turn-local messages[-1] split.
  • Gao et al., ALCE: Generating Text with Citations (EMNLP, 2023) ↗ & PhysioNet ArchEHR-QA (BioNLP 2025) ↗ · Inline bracketed [N] evidence grounding over EHR records.
  • HL7 AI Transparency on FHIR (AI-Provenance) ↗ & Amershi et al. (CHI, 2019) / Microsoft HAX ↗ · Progressive source disclosure and explicit human-review signaling.
Cross-Cutting · Blinded Evaluation & Claim Entailment

Why we verify outside the model with physician rubrics, atomic entailment, and two-judge consensus

Recent clinical AI evaluations show that LLM self-verification accepts incorrect medical answers over 60% of the time (Jin et al., 2026 ↗). Instead of asking the answering model to grade itself, the OpenClinAI Validation Harness → combines deterministic budget/citation checks with atomic EHR fact verification and two independent blinded judges:

  • HealthBench (OpenAI, 2025) ↗ & Stanford MedHELM ↗ · Multi-axis rubric scoring with strict-majority clinical harm consensus across published WAHS benchmark packets ↗.
  • Chung et al., VeriFact (2025) ↗ & Tang et al., MiniCheck (EMNLP, 2024) ↗ · Atomic claim-to-EHR entailment checking so cited [N] records genuinely support stated values, dates, and statuses.
  • Angelopoulos et al., Prediction-Powered Inference (Science, 2023) ↗ · Statistical framework for calibrating automated judge scores against clinician adjudication, paired with interactive inspection in the WAHS Explorer Sandbox ↗.

Functional audit

Verified subsystem guarantees across wahs_core and wahs_engine

Inside the repository, a pure-stdlib clinical library (src/wahs_core/, ~9.2k lines of Python + 5 offline rule packs) handles record cleaning and clinical logic, while a thin FastAPI wrapper (src/wahs_engine/, ~4.7k lines) handles authentication, byte-bounded caching, exact token counting, and streaming.

1. Record intake, condensation, and pre-generation clinical checks (wahs_core)

Verified on main

2-stage monotonic condensation

  • Predictable shrinking: Compresses 18 MB / 7,000-entry exports into exact model budgets (512 to 131,072 tokens; 16,384 ceiling by default) so expanding a budget never drops a record kept at a smaller budget.
  • Anti-starvation quotas: Reserves primary slots per clinical section so thousands of routine lab rows cannot crowd out discharge notes or care plans.
  • Hold-clause splicing: Preserves medication-hold instructions buried at the end of long hospital notes even when notes are excerpted.
Verified on main

Clinical graph & temporal digest

  • 7 relationship types: Connects medications, conditions, unit-separated labs, allergies, vaccines, notes, and clinics to flag allergy cross-reactivity, renal/potassium hazards (preserving electrolyte salts), CKD and NYHA stage conflicts, and word-boundary note holds.
  • Tie-safe timelines: Computes a clean reference_date from completed visits, separates on-hold from stopped prescriptions, and supports multi-day mixed-timezone trends while refusing same-day timestamp ties.
Hardened in PR #12

FHIR R4 statuses & fail-closed privacy

  • Invalidated status filtering: Excludes entered-in-error allergies, meds, procedures, notes, immunizations, child panel labs, and appointments; demotes refuted allergies and omits cancelled/noshow visits from upcoming care.
  • Conclusions, dosages & codings: Extracts DiagnosticReport.conclusion, list-valued MedicationStatement.dosage[], and prefers RxNorm/SNOMED CT/LOINC/CVX across multi-coded resources (US Core ServiceRequest and QuestionnaireResponse remain future Phase 2 extensions).
  • Defense-in-depth redaction: Scans reasons, notes, referenced conditions, and clinic names at extraction + lookup time, fails closed on invalid sharing_posture values, and covers uncoded MOUD, HIV ARV, and WA MHMDA lexical terms.

2. Per-patient caching, token sizing, and live streaming (wahs_engine)

Verified on main (808d16a)

Live freshness, JWKS defense-in-depth & exact tokenizers

  • Checks fhir-auth on every turn: Verifies MasterPatient ownership and linked clinic dataset meta.lastUpdated stamps before reading byte-bounded LRU caches, blocks internal HAPI bypass URLs via parsed hostname checks, and supports optional local pure-stdlib RS256 JWKS verification.
  • Offline token accuracy: Bundled Gemma 4 and tiktoken tokenizers match GPU prompt token counts within <0.01% completely offline, enforcing model ceilings on @<budget> suffixes and trimming oldest chat turns first.
Hardened in PR #10 & PR #12

Streaming preflight, worker threads & SDK compatibility

  • Preflight status guarantees: Validates prompt budgets and opens the initial upstream LLM stream before committing HTTP 200 SSE headers so invalid turns or upstream outages return clean HTTP 400, 422, or 502 responses, and strips unclosed <think> reasoning on length cutoffs.
  • Live lookahead + vanilla OpenAI mode: Streams tokens live after a 96-character lookahead for WAHS BFFs while supporting wahs.emit_stream_events: false for standard OpenAI SDKs, pooling HTTP connections, and offloading CPU condensation/BM25 via asyncio.to_thread.

Code audit & remediation ledger

All 27 audit items verified on main (808d16a)

Reviewing all 18 Python modules and 5 clinical rule packs identified 27 concrete engineering items across wahs_engine (E1–E11) and wahs_core (C1–C10). All four remediation waves are now merged and regression-tested on main, keeping baseline fixes cleanly separated from future out-of-scope behavioral experiments.

  1. Merged 12-persona stack & runtime pooling fixes on wahs-engine#10 Verified · PR #10

    Shared an app-level httpx.AsyncClient pool so single-flight dataset coalescing never closes a disconnected request's socket under a concurrent caller (E1), capped @<budget> suffixes at 131,072 tokens / model context windows (E2), wrote refreshed datasets back into _dataset_cache on refresh=True (E3), and resolved cross-key model aliases and Qwen tokenizers (E10).

  2. Hardened wahs_core clinical, FHIR R4 status, and privacy invariants Verified · PR #12

    Scanned reasons, referenced conditions, notes, and clinic names at extraction and tool-lookup time (C1), enforced fail-closed validate_sharing_posture() with expanded MOUD/ARV/WA MHMDA lexical terms (C2), filtered entered-in-error and not-done statuses across allergies, procedures, child panel labs, notes, and appointments (C3), extracted DiagnosticReport.conclusion and list-valued dosages (C4), preserved electrolyte salts (C5), hardened word-boundary note cues and directional low-HbA1c rules (C6), separated trajectory units and on-hold meds (C7–C9), and locked concurrent FTS5 queries (C10).

  3. Hardened streaming preflight, JWKS defense-in-depth, and worker offload Verified · PR #12

    Added zero-dependency RFC 8017 RS256 JWKS verification, urlsplit internal-HAPI hostname blocking, and explicit MasterPatient ownership checks (E4), stripped unclosed <think> blocks on length cutoffs (E5), validated prompt budgets and Round-0 upstream streams before committing HTTP 200 SSE headers (E6), added wahs.emit_stream_events: false for vanilla OpenAI SDKs and guarded malformed tool JSON (E7–E8), and offloaded CPU condensation/BM25 via asyncio.to_thread with byte-bounded LRU caches and full OpenAI parameter forwarding (E9, E11).

  4. Synchronized connected frontends, harness, and reference docs Verified · All Merged

    Merged shl-ips#2/#3, wahs-mobile-frontend#2/#3, validation-harness#219, and openclinai.org documentation so repository docs, API contracts, and public architecture pages reflect the exact verified baseline ahead of UW CIRG team strategy sync and EHIgnite Phase 2 partner stress testing.
