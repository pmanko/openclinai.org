Washington Health Summary (WAHS) · Current-State Audit

How the WAHS engine works right now.

The WAHS Engine is a small, standalone service that sits between the WA Health Summary app, the patient's health record store, and an open-weight language model. This report audits how the engine is built today, links the clinical and privacy foundations behind it, and lists what is solid versus what still needs fixing.

Audited 19 November 2026 · wahs-engine on GitHub ↗ (main at 85fb799 & open PR #10) · Automated suites: 81 / 86 tests passing

Where things stand today

  1. The core architecture is working and cleanly separated. The engine shrinks large multi-clinic FHIR records by 90–99%, checks drug conflicts and lab trends in plain Python before calling AI, and maps every cited claim back to its original record.
  2. Condensing first beats iterative AI tool-calling on both quality and safety. Across 900 graded runs on an NVIDIA H200, Gemma 4 26B with a 16k condensed chart + clinical rules scored 85.1 / 100 with zero confirmed unsafe answers (3.4s median wait), whereas forcing models to search a 2k summary via tool calls dropped scores to 65.8–69.4 and produced two-thirds of all unsafe answers.
  3. A short, concrete list of edge cases needs fixing before merging the 12-patient demo stack. Our code audit found 3 runtime bugs on open PR #10 (HTTP client lifecycle, uncapped budget suffixes, and manual refresh cache updates) plus targeted gaps in filtering entered-in-error records and uncoded sensitive medications.

High-level architecture

How a patient question moves through the system

Three services share the work so each stays simple and replaceable: the patient app handles sign-in and conversation UI, the WAHS Engine prepares the patient's record and clinical checks, and a stateless model server writes the plain-language answer.

Research, standards & best-practice foundations

Why each step in the engine exists, and the sources behind it

Each of the four steps in the diagram above (plus our evaluation harness) implements established findings from long-context LLM research, healthcare interoperability standards, clinical practice guidelines, and privacy law.

Step 1 · Fetch & Check Freshness

Patient-mediated FHIR APIs and version-stamped caching

Patients gather records across clinics through Cures Act FHIR R4 APIs. Checking lightweight meta.lastUpdated version stamps before pulling full bundles keeps charts fresh in ~10 ms when nothing changed.

  • HL7 US Core (FHIR R4) ↗ & ONC Cures Act Final Rule ↗ · Standard patient-access clinical profiles and Patient/$everything retrieval.
  • SMART Health Links ↗ & HL7 FHIR DS4P Security Labels ↗ · Patient-mediated sharing and meta.security access scoping via uwcirg/jwt-proxy ↗.
  • HL7 FHIR RESTful Versioning (meta.lastUpdated) ↗ · Optimistic cache revalidation across multi-clinic datasets (session & scope design →).
Step 2 · Condense Record (90–99%)

Long-context attention limits and clinical safety priority

Language models lose recall when key facts sit buried in noisy multi-megabyte JSON. Pinning an international clinical safety core first and explicitly logging omitted counts prevents silent truncation and guessing.

  • Liu et al., Lost in the Middle (TACL, 2024) ↗ · Demonstrates U-shaped long-context recall degradation, motivating compact 16k condensation over raw 200k+ FHIR dumps.
  • HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) & ISO 27269 ↗ · Defines the mandatory safety core (allergies, active meds, active problems, implants) preserved first across all budgets.
  • Robertson & Zaragoza, Okapi BM25 (2009) ↗ · Grounds exact clinical-term passage retrieval (note_rag) when long hospital notes exceed budget.
Step 3 · Clinical & Privacy Rules

Symbolic clinical reconciliation and data-layer redaction

Cross-clinic discrepancies (such as an urgent-care note holding an outpatient pill) are safety signals to surface, and sensitive-record exclusions belong in deterministic code before prompt assembly.

  • HL7 Clinical Information Reconciliation (CIRI) ↗ & WHO SMART Guidelines ↗ · Computable clinical reconciliation ahead of prose generation (Med Agent Hub →).
  • KDIGO 2024 CKD ↗, ADA Diabetes ↗, NLM RxNorm ↗, LOINC ↗ / UCUM ↗ & CDC ACIP ↗ · Renal/potassium cutoffs, active-ingredient matching, unit conversion, and vaccine intervals.
  • 42 CFR Part 2 ↗ & WA My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373) ↗ · Statutory basis for data-layer external_share redaction and scope-isolated chat sessions.
Step 4 · Split Prompt & Attach Links

Prefix KV-cache reuse, inline citations, and human review

Keeping messages[0] identical across questions lets GPU servers reuse cached attention blocks (cutting follow-ups from ~19s to ~3s), while [1]..[N] source links make every claim inspectable.

  • Kwon et al., PagedAttention / vLLM (SOSP, 2023) ↗ & Zheng et al., SGLang / RadixAttention (NeurIPS, 2024) ↗ · Prefix KV-cache reuse motivating the invariant messages[0] / turn-local messages[-1] split.
  • Gao et al., ALCE: Generating Text with Citations (EMNLP, 2023) ↗ & PhysioNet ArchEHR-QA (BioNLP 2025) ↗ · Inline bracketed [N] evidence grounding over EHR records.
  • HL7 AI Transparency on FHIR (AI-Provenance) ↗ & Amershi et al. (CHI, 2019) / Microsoft HAX ↗ · Progressive source disclosure and explicit human-review signaling.
Cross-Cutting · Blinded Evaluation & Claim Entailment

Why we verify outside the model with physician rubrics, atomic entailment, and two-judge consensus

Recent clinical AI evaluations show that LLM self-verification accepts incorrect medical answers over 60% of the time (Jin et al., 2026 ↗). Instead of asking the answering model to grade itself, the OpenClinAI Validation Harness → combines deterministic budget/citation checks with atomic EHR fact verification and two independent blinded judges:

  • HealthBench (OpenAI, 2025) ↗ & Stanford MedHELM ↗ · Multi-axis rubric scoring with strict-majority clinical harm consensus across published WAHS benchmark packets ↗.
  • Chung et al., VeriFact (2025) ↗ & Tang et al., MiniCheck (EMNLP, 2024) ↗ · Atomic claim-to-EHR entailment checking so cited [N] records genuinely support stated values, dates, and statuses.
  • Angelopoulos et al., Prediction-Powered Inference (Science, 2023) ↗ · Statistical framework for calibrating automated judge scores against clinician adjudication, paired with interactive inspection in the WAHS Explorer Sandbox ↗.

Functional audit

What works well today vs. where current gaps sit

Inside the repository, a pure-stdlib clinical library (src/wahs_core/, ~8.9k lines of Python + 5 offline rule packs) handles record cleaning and clinical logic, while a thin FastAPI wrapper (src/wahs_engine/, ~4.4k lines) handles authentication, caching, exact token counting, and streaming.

1. Record intake, condensation, and pre-generation clinical checks (wahs_core)

Solid Today

2-stage monotonic condensation

  • Predictable shrinking: Compresses 18 MB / 7,000-entry exports into exact model budgets (512 to 131,072 tokens; 16,384 ceiling by default) so expanding a budget never drops a record kept at a smaller budget.
  • Anti-starvation quotas: Reserves primary slots per clinical section so thousands of routine lab rows cannot crowd out discharge notes or care plans.
  • Hold-clause splicing: Preserves medication-hold instructions buried at the end of long hospital notes even when notes are excerpted.
Solid Today

Clinical graph & temporal digest

  • 7 relationship types: Connects medications, conditions, labs, allergies, vaccines, notes, and clinics to flag allergy cross-reactivity, renal/potassium hazards (with UCUM unit conversion), duplicate therapies, and note-vs-pill-list holds.
  • Tie-safe timelines: Computes a clean reference_date from completed visits (ignoring future care-plan end dates) and refuses to guess lab trend directions when same-day timestamps tie.
Gaps Found in Audit

Record statuses & uncoded sensitivity

  • Erroneous statuses: Active conditions filter entered-in-error, but erroneous or not-done allergies, procedures, notes, and cancelled appointments can still appear as clinical or planned events.
  • Dropped fields & types: DiagnosticReport.conclusion and list-valued MedicationStatement.dosage are skipped; US Core ServiceRequest and QuestionnaireResponse are not yet extracted.
  • Uncoded drugs: When EHRs omit security tags, lexical fallback misses uncoded buprenorphine/MOUD and HIV antiretrovirals during external sharing.

2. Per-patient caching, token sizing, and live streaming (wahs_engine)

Solid on main (85fb799)

Live freshness checks & exact offline tokenizers

  • Checks fhir-auth on every turn: Verifies MasterPatient and linked clinic dataset meta.lastUpdated stamps before reading in-memory caches, and blocks truncated external shares with HTTP 409.
  • Offline token accuracy: Bundled Gemma 4 and tiktoken tokenizers match GPU prompt token counts within <0.01% completely offline, trimming oldest chat turns first when conversations grow long.
Runtime Caveats & Open PR #10

Streaming buffering, memory bounds, and proxy reliance

  • Tool-mode streaming: On main, attaching overflow lookup tools buffers the first model pass before streaming tokens; open PR #10 upgrades this to live streaming after a 96-character lookahead.
  • Defense in depth: wahs-engine relies on upstream fhir-auth for RS256 JWT signature verification rather than checking JWKS locally, and runs CPU condensation on the main async loop thread with entry-count (rather than byte-size) cache caps.

Code audit findings & action plan

What our deep audit found to fix next

Reviewing all 18 Python modules and 5 rule packs surfaced 27 concrete engineering items. Rather than a flat bug list, the four steps below sequence them by real-world risk, followed by expandable file-and-line tables for implementers.

  1. Fix 3 regressions on wahs-engine PR #10 before merging the 12-patient stack Priority 1 · Blocker

    Use a shared app-level HTTP client pool so single-flight dataset coalescing cannot close a disconnected request's socket under a concurrent request (E1); cap @<budget> model suffixes so a large number cannot allocate unbounded GPU memory (E2); and write refreshed datasets back into _dataset_cache on refresh=True (E3).

  2. Tighten sensitive-record redaction and invalidated FHIR status filters in wahs_core Priority 2 · Clinical & Privacy

    Scan medication/procedure/goal reasons and clinic names at extraction time so lookup tools never leak sensitive entries during external sharing (C1), expand uncoded MOUD/HIV medication patterns and fail closed on unknown posture strings (C2), filter out entered-in-error and not-done allergies, procedures, child panel labs, and appointments (C3), and extract DiagnosticReport.conclusion plus list-valued MedicationStatement.dosage (C4).

  3. Harden streaming error handling, truncated reasoning stripping, and local JWT defense-in-depth Priority 3 · Runtime

    Strip unclosed <think> blocks when output hits max_tokens (E5), validate prompt budgets before sending HTTP 200 SSE headers (E6), add optional local Keycloak JWKS signature verification alongside fhir-auth (E4), and move CPU-heavy condensation onto worker threads with byte-bounded caches (E9).

  4. Finish aligning downstream callers and reference docs Priority 4 · Cleanup

    Merge shl-ips#2 and wahs-mobile-frontend#2 once Step 1 lands, point Med Agent Hub's local clinical_graph.py copy back to shared wahs_core, and sync internal doc files with the current 7-edge graph and tokenizer dependencies.
