WITH WAHS EXAMPLE EXPORTS

Apply the approach first

A simple file reader can expose entries with their source, date and original content. That may be enough for a small pilot.

The current QueryStore module is built for OpenMRS. Support for arbitrary WAHS FHIR exports would need to be designed and tested.

Keep the limits visible. A searchable copy can be incomplete or out of date. Version markers help detect changes; a result count does not prove completeness. Patient access controls need their own design. QueryStore also has maintenance operations, so the pilot should expose only the intended reading functions. Its index repair is different from reconciling clinical meaning.

Help people find the entries behind an answer

The notes propose a first version of search-assisted answering, often called RAG. The useful pattern is simple: prepare readable entries, find relevant ones, then answer with their sources visible.

  1. 01

    Prepare

    Keep names, values, units, dates and source links intact.

  2. 02

    Find

    Read a small case in full or search a larger record.

  3. 03

    Answer

    Use the selected entries and show what supports the answer.

  4. 04

    Inspect

    Open the source, spot an omission and ask again.

What QueryStore already provides

In the reviewed OpenMRS code, QueryStore turns clinical objects into compact readable text with metadata and source identifiers. An embedding is a numerical representation used to find similar text; it does not replace the source record.

The Elasticsearch path stores vectors alongside record content and can search using an embedding of the question. Other search backends also exist. Which embedding model runs depends on configuration.

The notes name E5-base-v2. The reviewed harness configuration names a different default, so the active model must be checked on the chosen server. The E5 model card describes English-only input and a limited input length; it does not establish multilingual support.

03 / HUMAN REVIEW WORKFLOW

Review, decide, then review again

Adapt Catalyst’s iterative review approach: AI and a person work through a flagged conflict together, revising the proposed resolution as context becomes clearer. Keep the original records and the history of decisions.

Proposed workflow · to explore with WAHS
  1. 01

    Flag

    AI identifies a possible conflict and shows its sources.

  2. 02

    Discuss

    The person adds context, asks questions or corrects an assumption.

  3. 03

    Propose

    AI suggests a resolution and explains what it would change.

  4. 04

    Refine

    The person and AI revise the proposal as often as needed.

  5. 05

    Decide

    The person accepts, edits, rejects or leaves it unresolved.

Return to the sources and discussion whenever a proposal needs more context. Record an accepted resolution separately from the originals, with its explanation and human decision. Unresolved is a valid outcome. Previewing and sharing the resulting view is a separate step.

ILLUSTRATIVE CASE

Two medication entries disagree

Source AEarlier dose recordedOriginal entry retained
Source BDifferent dose recordedOriginal entry retained

The difference may be a dose change, a duplicate import, or a real disagreement. A patient can state what they currently take. That statement does not silently replace the prescription. AI might then propose treating the entries as a change over time, if the dates and supporting records justify it. The person can correct that interpretation, request another revision or leave the difference unresolved.

Concept illustration; not a patient record or clinical recommendation.

DESIGN REQUIREMENTS TO AGREE

Keep the person in control

  • Make the proposed change and affected fields visible.
  • Let the person add context and request another revision.
  • Retain accept, edit, reject and defer as distinct decisions.
  • Keep the original entry and the version the person reviewed.
  • Ask for a fresh review if relevant information changes.
  • Preserve user input when a save fails.
  • Preview sharing separately from editing.

08 / RESEARCH & STANDARDS FOUNDATION

Research-based backing, clinical standards & regulatory architecture

Why patient-mediated multi-clinic summaries cannot rely on raw FHIR prompting alone—and how health interoperability standards, clinical practice guidelines, privacy law, and human-AI interaction research ground each layer of the WAHS+ architecture.

1 · LONG-CONTEXT CLINICAL NLP & IPS PRIORITIZATION

Why deterministic condensation must precede model inference

Empirical evaluations of frontier language models over long clinical documents consistently demonstrate position-bias degradation (“Lost in the Middle”; Liu et al., TACL 2024 ↗) and severe token inflation when serialized FHIR R4 JSON is supplied directly. In our synthetic cohort, an 84-year longitudinal record (Maurice742_Auer97) spans 18.36 MB and ~3.52M raw tokens—17× to 27× larger than 128k–200k context windows—where over 95% of tokens consist of repeated Base64 encounter notes, administrative claims (Claim / ExplanationOfBenefit), and serial laboratory flowsheets.

To prevent ad-hoc truncation from dropping active allergies or cross-clinic medications, our 5-Tier Condenser implements the priority hierarchy of the HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) and ISO 27269:

  • Mandatory IPS Safety Core (Protected across Tiers 1–4): Allergies and intolerances, active medications, and active problem-list conditions are never truncated, even at the 1,024-token emergency tier.
  • SHA-256 Note Payload Deduplication: Eliminates copy-forwarded and dual-indexed Base64 clinical notes across DocumentReference and DiagnosticReport.presentedForm[] while retaining unique clinical narratives.
  • Longitudinal LOINC Trajectory Folding: Collapses thousands of serial numeric observations into canonical per-analyte chains (Initial, Latest, Min/Max, Count, and Trend Direction) with preserved member UUID lists.

2 · MULTI-SOURCE RECONCILIATION & CLINICAL ONTOLOGIES

Deterministic terminology normalization paired with LLM synthesis

When patients aggregate records across unaffiliated clinics via SMART Health Links ↗ or TEFCA patient-access exchange, semantic heterogeneity causes unassisted language models to miss critical safety conflicts: one clinic prescribes brand Plavix 75 mg while another lists generic Clopidogrel 75 mg; primary care retains CKD Stage 1, 2, and 3 simultaneously active while serial eGFR labs demonstrate progression; or retail pharmacy and state registry (WA IIS) exports report overlapping immunizations.

Our 4-Layer Clinical Knowledge Base executes deterministic ontology and guideline checks prior to narrative generation:

  • Layer 1 — SNOMED CT & Disease Stage Exclusivity: Applies KDIGO 2024 CKD Guidelines ↗ and ADA Standards of Care ↗ to detect mutually exclusive active disease stages and uncoded renal/glycemic progression.
  • Layer 2 — RxNorm & DailyMed Medication Safety: Maps brand and generic prescriptions to canonical NLM RxNorm ↗ ingredients and therapeutic classes (DailyMed / MedlinePlus ↗), flagging duplicate antiplatelet/statin/ACE-inhibitor therapy and active-vs-stopped contradictions.
  • Layer 3 — CDC ACIP & LOINC Harmonization: Cross-checks vaccine CVX codes against CDC ACIP schedules ↗ and merges blood-versus-serum LOINC ↗ codes (e.g., 2339-0 vs. 2345-7 for glucose).
3 · DATA SEGMENTATION FOR PRIVACY (DS4P) & STATE/FEDERAL LAW

Enforcing patient_self vs. external_share before condensation

Modern EHR problem lists and observation flowsheets routinely store sensitive Social Determinants of Health (SDOH), substance-use screening (NIDA DAST-10 ↗), intimate partner violence (SNOMED 706893006), and justice-involvement findings alongside medical diagnoses. If an LLM summarizes the raw bundle first and attempts to filter sensitive topics only in the output prompt, sensitive context can leak into clinical reasoning or citations.

Layer 4 of our architecture implements pre-condensation data segmentation aligned with federal and Washington state privacy frameworks:

  • 42 CFR Part 2 & Behavioral Health Confidentiality: Protects substance-use disorder screening and treatment records from unauthorized redisclosure (42 CFR Part 2 ↗).
  • Washington State My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373): Establishes strict consumer consent requirements for sharing Washington residents’ health data (WA RCW 19.373 ↗).
  • HL7 FHIR DS4P & Gravity Project SDOH Taxonomy: In patient_self mode, sensitive items remain visible with explicit privacy badges so the patient knows what is in their chart; in external_share mode, sensitive resources are stripped deterministically before Tier 1–4 condensation, guaranteeing 0.0% token or UUID leakage (ONC Privacy Guidance ↗).
4 · VERIFIABLE AI PROVENANCE & HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP AGENCY

Three-layer transparency and calibration of trust

Clinical decision-support research shows that fluent AI explanations can induce over-reliance unless users can verify source records directly and distinguish automated checks from human clinical sign-off. Our UX and data model synthesize three complementary standards:

  • HL7 AI Transparency on FHIR (AI-Provenance): Distinguishes (1) supporting chart records ([N] → FHIR resourceUuid), (2) the AI system and condensation tier that transformed them, and (3) recorded human review or patient annotation (HL7 AI-Provenance Profile ↗).
  • Microsoft HAX Guidelines (G9 Correction & G11 Explainability): Keeps original FHIR resources immutable, supports accept/edit/reject/defer conflict workflows, and surfaces the exact clinical rule behind each flag (Microsoft HAX Toolkit ↗).
  • Progressive Disclosure & Explicit Abstention: Following NN/g Progressive Disclosure ↗ and W3C WCAG 2.1 ↗, the interactive prototype leads users through four focused steps and abstains 100% of the time when queried about undocumented clinical facts.

9.1 Candid Audit of What We Built & Up-to-Date Evaluation Methodology

CRITICAL AUDIT OF PHASE 2

Four methodological gaps in our current prototype & benchmark

  • Deterministic Verification vs. Live Generative LLM Evaluation: Our Phase 2 evaluator (wahs_analyzer.py) tests deterministic Python normalization and structured templates over condensed FHIR ledgers rather than live open-weight LLM generation (Gemma 4, Qwen 3, MedGemma). Thus, the 99.4 / 100 headline score validates our pre-LLM condensation and rule engine, not generative model fidelity. Meanwhile, the 61.2 / 100 Opus 5.5 row is a reconstructed reference baseline from Phase 1 observations over manually sliced JSON rather than a live side-by-side harness run over all 32 prompts.
  • Co-Designed Perturbations & Rule Tables (Ceiling Effect): The 15 multi-clinic conflicts in Set B and the 4-Layer lookup tables were co-designed in the same sprint over the exact SNOMED/RxNorm/CVX/LOINC codes present in the three test bundles. Because generator and detector share a finite code vocabulary, recall hits 100% by construction.
  • Clean Synthea Regularity & Tiny Cohort (N = 3): Three clean Synthea patients have 100% coded entries (2,629 / 2,629). Real multi-EHR patient exports and Epic EHI dumps contain uncoded free-text problems, local charge codes, OCR/scanned PDF notes, and garbled sig strings where deterministic lookup alone drops sharply.
  • Evaluator Schema Drift & Jargon: An audit between our in-process report script (32 / 32) and standalone HTTP harness runner (summary.json, 81 / 96 cells) exposed a field-name mismatch (abstained vs. abstains) and a substring false positive where the redaction banner triggered the forbidden-term check. We also remediated opaque metrics (e.g., “4-Layer KB Code Coverage” → Medical Code Recognition Rate) across the published evaluation report.
MODERN EVALUATION METHODOLOGY

Grounding Phase 3 in AI research, industry rubrics & shared tooling

  • OpenAI HealthBench (2025) Weighted Physician Rubrics: Replaces binary pass/fail keyword checks with instance-specific, clinician-authored rubric criteria carrying positive weights for required clinical context/actions and negative penalties for dangerous omissions, false reassurance, or clinical jargon in patient answers (OpenAI HealthBench ↗).
  • Stanford MedHELM & MedAgentBench: Evaluates real-world EHR decision-support and multi-step FHIR agent workflows rather than multiple-choice licensing exams (Stanford MedHELM ↗).
  • VeriFact (2025), FActScore & MiniCheck Atomic Entailment: Decomposes every LLM response into atomic clinical propositions and verifies via Natural Language Inference (NLI) that each cited [N] → FHIR resourceUuid actually entails the claim—catching citation distortion where a model cites a real lab or note while misstating its value or date.
  • Shared Open Tooling & OpenClinAI Blind Judging: Integrates Promptfoo ↗ (declarative prompt/model matrices & red-teaming), DeepEval / Ragas ↗ (faithfulness & context recall), and our blind multi-judge protocol (Claude Opus 5.5 + GPT-6-Astra with stripped check labels).

9.4 Prompt Engineering & Orchestrated Agent Teams for KB-Based Queries

PROMPT ENGINEERING FOR SMALL/MIDSIZE MODELS

Five empirically validated prompting levers

  • 1. Pre-Injected Temporal & Reference-Date Anchor: Always supply the chart’s reference date and pre-computed relative intervals (e.g., [2023-11-20 · 2y 11m ago]) so 4B–14B models never perform raw calendar arithmetic.
  • 2. Deterministic KB Fact Sheet Pre-Attachment: Inject matched 4-Layer KB findings (RxNorm ingredient matches, KDIGO eGFR stage rules, ACIP intervals) as an explicit [CLINICAL RULE ALERTS] block above the condensed ledger so the LLM explains verified findings rather than memorizing ontology tables.
  • 3. Constrained [N] Bracket Citation Grammar: Require every clinical assertion to carry an inline [N] index from the condensed ledger or explicitly state “Not documented in the provided health summary.”
  • 4. Evidence-First Ordering & Sampling Control: Place the condensed ledger at the top of the prompt (maximizing prefix-cache hits), repeat the user question at the bottom, and disable aggressive repetition penalties (dry_multiplier = 0, temperature = 0.0–0.2) that corrupt medication lists.
  • 5. Audience Dual-Register Prompting: Separate Patient Mode (6th–8th grade plain English, empowering questions to ask the care team) from Clinician Mode (problem-oriented assessment & plan with LOINC/RxNorm codes).
ORCHESTRATED AGENT TEAM EXPERIMENTS

When do multi-agent teams beat a single model on KB queries?

Drawing on our med-agent-hub findings (specs/checked-answers.md), letting a second agent blindly rewrite a draft often deletes valid clinical details (verifier over-rejection). Instead, Phase 3 tests four controlled architectures on KB-heavy queries:

  • Arm 1 — Single Model + Raw Condensed Ledger: Baseline single pass without KB rule pre-injection.
  • Arm 2 — Single Model + Deterministic 4-Layer KB Pre-Injection: Single LLM call augmented with deterministic RxNorm/SNOMED/ACIP/LOINC rule outputs.
  • Arm 3 — Cross-Family Label-Only Checked Pipeline (single-12b-xcheck): Writer (Gemma 4 12B) drafts the answer; a cross-family Reviewer (Qwen 3.5 9B or Granite 4.2 8B) and narrow Entailment Grounder check each claim against the KB and cited FHIR records, attaching verification badges or triggering a single targeted repair pass.
  • Arm 4 — Orchestrated Specialist KB Team (team-wahs-kb): A lightweight Router dispatches parallel domain specialists—Medication & RxNorm Specialist, Lab & KDIGO/ADA Trend Specialist, and ACIP Immunization & Care-Gap Specialist—followed by a Synthesizer and Privacy/Provenance Gatekeeper.

9.5 Evaluating Condensation Tiers & Keeping the Full Chart Queryable

Condensing an 18.4 MB chart down to 2k–8k tokens guarantees that the IPS Safety Core (active allergies, active meds, active problems, recent labs) is always in context, but aggressive condensation (Tier 3/4) intentionally drops older encounters, historical stopped medications, and full free-text clinical notes. To evaluate information loss across tiers and keep 100% of the full chart queryable even for small-context models, Phase 3 introduces a Two-Tier Hybrid Memory Architecture:

HYBRID FULL-CHART QUERYABILITY

Always-On IPS Core + On-Demand Tier 0/1 Retrieval Tools

  1. 01

    Always-In-Context Safety Core (1k–4k tokens)

    Every prompt includes the Tier 3/4 condensed IPS summary so the model never misses an active allergy, active drug conflict, or chronic condition.

  2. 02

    Scoped MCP / Function-Calling Chart Tools

    When a question asks about historical details omitted from the condensed view (e.g., “Why was Furosemide stopped in 2022?” or “What did my 2019 cardiology note say?”), the model calls read-only FHIR retrieval tools: search_chart_entries(query, resource_types, date_range), get_lab_history(loinc_code), and read_clinical_note(document_uuid).

  3. 03

    Ephemeral Client/Server Embeddings

    Indexes Tier 0/1 overflow entries in an ephemeral in-memory vector index using nomic-embed-text (on the CIRG LiteLLM endpoint) or browser-side Transformers.js, returning top-k historical FHIR entries with exact [N] → resourceUuid provenance.

5×4 CONDENSATION ABLATION EXPERIMENT

How condensation interacts with model selection

We will evaluate a 5 (Condensation Configurations: Tier 1 16k, Tier 2 8k, Tier 3 4k, Tier 4 2k static, and Tier 4 2k + On-Demand Retrieval) × 4 (Model Classes: 4B, 9B–12B, 27B–31B, 35B-A3B MoE) factorial matrix across four question horizons:

  • Current Active State Queries: Expected flat performance from Tier 1 to Tier 4 (100% answered from the IPS Safety Core).
  • 24-Month Trend & Conflict Queries: High accuracy at Tiers 1–3; slight dip at static Tier 4 recovered by Hybrid Retrieval.
  • Deep Historical Queries (>2 Years Ago): Sharp drop at static Tiers 3–4 (by design), testing whether Tier 4 (2k) + On-Demand Tool Retrieval recovers >95% of full-chart recall at one-fifth the token cost.
  • Model Selection Implication: Static Tier 3/4 summaries only require strong reading comprehension (enabling 4B–8B models), whereas Hybrid Full-Chart Queryability requires reliable tool-calling (favoring Qwen 3 8B/27B, Gemma 4 12B/31B, or Granite 4.2 8B).

9.6 Privacy Implementation & Encrypting/Obfuscating Specific Parts of a FHIR Bundle

How does WAHS+’s privacy segmentation connect to prior cryptographic FHIR privacy work across UW DIGI and CIRG? Our review links three complementary mechanisms—including Ian Bacher’s proof-of-concept in DIGI-UW/ips-selective-disclosures (sd-fhir) ↗ and uwcirg/shl-ips ↗—into a unified Three-Layer FHIR Privacy & Selective-Disclosure Architecture:

PRIOR UW DIGI & CIRG CRYPTOGRAPHIC WORK

Element- & Entry-Level Obfuscation via sd-fhir (SD-JWT) and SHL (JWE)

  • 1. IETF SD-JWT Selective Disclosure on FHIR (DIGI-UW/ips-selective-disclosures): In Ian Bacher’s sd-fhir package, any sensitive FHIR resource in Bundle.entry[] or primitive field (e.g., Patient.address, Patient.telecom, or a sensitive Condition) marked with extension http://smart.who.int/icvp/StructureDefinition/SelectiveDisclosure is extracted by buildDisclosureFrame(). In the shared FHIR JSON, the sensitive element is replaced in-place by a salted SHA-256/SHA-512 cryptographic digest stored inside an http://smart.who.int/icvp/Extension/sd-hash extension (valueBase64Binary), while the underlying [salt, key, value] disclosures travel separately in a companion fhirR4.Parameters resource. This keeps the redacted FHIR Bundle 100% schema-valid and allows an issuer’s signature to be verified without revealing the obfuscated entries!
  • 2. SMART Health Links Payload & Sub-Bundle Encryption (uwcirg/shl-ips): Uses JSON Web Encryption (JWE, RFC 7516, A256GCM) where the 256-bit decryption key is carried in the client-side URL fragment (shlink:/...) and never sent to the hosting server. High-sensitivity resources can also be wrapped in a separately encrypted JWE sub-bundle or _extension unlocked only with a secondary recipient key or patient passcode.
  • 3. QueryStore Read-Projection Redaction & HL7 DS4P: Aligns with QueryStore’s defense-in-depth read-boundary projection and HL7 FHIR Data Segmentation for Privacy (meta.security labels for 42 CFR Part 2, SDOH, and WA MHMDA).
UNIFIED WAHS+ PRIVACY PIPELINE

How all three layers work together in Phase 3

  1. 01

    Tag (DS4P + 4-Layer KB)

    Automatically tag sensitive FHIR resources and fields (SDOH, IPV, substance use, behavioral/reproductive health) with meta.security and SelectiveDisclosure extensions.

  2. 02

    Gate Before LLM Inference

    In external_share mode, strip withheld resources before Tier 1–4 condensation and LLM prompting so sensitive facts never enter model context or embeddings.

  3. 03

    Obfuscate in Exported FHIR (sd-fhir)

    Replace withheld Bundle.entry[] resources or fields with salted sd-hash extensions so the exported IPS Bundle remains FHIR-valid and cryptographically verifiable without exposing the hidden text.

  4. 04

    Encrypt for Sharing (SHL JWE)

    Package the selective-disclosure IPS Bundle into a JWE-encrypted SMART Health Link (uwcirg/shl-ips) with recipient-scoped disclosure parameters.
