WA Health Summary (WAHS) · Interactive Tour & Manual Testing Guide

WAHS Patient Chart Explorer — Walkthrough & Experimentation Guide.

The WAHS Patient Chart Explorer is both an interactive demonstration of patient-mediated FHIR R4 / IPS chart condensation and a live experimentation workbench. Every choice you make in Step 1 (Patient & Raw FHIR Chart) feeds into Step 2 (Condense & Prepare Chart), and the exact condensed chart you configure in Step 2 flows directly into Step 3 (Clinical Tasks & Grounded Q&A) for live testing against local open-weight models, curated Med Agent Hub profiles, or cloud models via OpenRouter.

Part 2 · Hands-On Experimentation Tutorial

Four reproducible recipes for manual testing & model comparison

Use these four step-by-step recipes in the interactive WAHS Explorer (or locally against `med-agent-hub` on `:18081` and `llama-router` on `:8077`) to test token-budget sensitivity, knowledge-base augmentation, cloud vs. local models, and safety abstention.

Recipe 1 · Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3

Compare an `8k` vs. `1k` condensed chart on a 7,280-entry oncology record

Test how aggressive token-budget condensation affects prompt size, latency, and historical recall on a massive multi-year chart.

  1. In Step 1, click Maurice Auer (7,280 FHIR entries · ~4.57M raw tokens).
  2. Go to Step 2, keep Lossless (Verbatim) active, and select the `8k` (`8,192` tok) budget (`159 lines`, `8,152 actual tokens`).
  3. Go to Step 3, select a clinical task or sample question, and click ⚡ Generate Live. Note the telemetry pill: Input: 8,152 tok (159 lines).
  4. Now return to Step 2, toggle the budget to `1k` (`1,024` tok), return to Step 3, and click ⚡ Generate Live again.
What to observe: At 1k, the input payload drops by 88% to Input: 944 tok (19 lines), cutting prefill latency (`TTFT`) dramatically while preserving Maurice’s active diagnoses, active medications, and allergies—whereas older longitudinal lab panels are listed in Step 2’s Omitted / Trimmed audit card.
Recipe 2 · Med Agent Hub Profiles

Test `wahs-12b-kb` (KB-Guided) vs. `wahs-12b-raw` (Unhinted Control)

Evaluate how much the deterministic 4-Layer Clinical Knowledge Base helps the language model catch cross-clinic conflicts and note-vs-order discrepancies.

  1. Select Elena Rostova (Age 81 · Cardiorenal HFrEF) in Step 1 and `8k` in Step 2.
  2. In Step 3, click the sample question chip: “Why did the hospital discharge note say to hold spironolactone and potassium chloride, and what were Elena’s potassium and eGFR levels?”
  3. In the top bar, choose `WAHS · Gemma 4 12B + 4-Layer Clinical KB (wahs-12b-kb)`, expand Inspect Active Hub Profile, and click Generate Live Response.
  4. Switch the dropdown to `WAHS · Gemma 4 12B Raw Condensed Chart Only (wahs-12b-raw)` (`include_kb_findings: false`) and click Generate Live Response again.
What to observe: Under wahs-12b-raw, the model must discover the contradiction between the active outpatient MedicationRequest [8, 9] and the free-text hospital discharge summary [17] purely from the raw condensed lines. Under wahs-12b-kb, Layer 2’s structured conflict finding is injected alongside the chart, ensuring consistent citation of both source records and a clear next-visit checklist.
Recipe 3 · OpenRouter Cloud Comparison

Compare local `llama-router` models against OpenRouter cloud models

Benchmark local open-weight models running on your workstation or CIRG GPU server against hosted cloud models (`Claude Sonnet 4`, `GPT-4.1`, or hosted `Gemma` / `Qwen`) on synthetic data.

  1. Click the ⚙ Configure Endpoint & OpenRouter Key button in the top navigation bar.
  2. Path A (Recommended via Med Agent Hub): Keep the endpoint set to http://127.0.0.1:18081/v1, paste your sk-or-v1-... key into the OpenRouter API Key field, and click Save & Probe. Hub automatically unlocks its `wahs-openrouter-*` profiles while applying the same WAHS system prompts (`wahs-kb-guided.txt` or `wahs-raw-unhinted.txt`).
  3. Path B (Direct Browser-to-OpenRouter): Click the OpenRouter Direct (`openrouter.ai`) preset button, enter your key, and click Save & Probe to query `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/chat/completions` directly from your browser.
What to observe: Compare `TTFT`, total generation speed, citation density (`[N]`), and plain-language clarity between local `wahs-12b-kb` and cloud `wahs-openrouter-sonnet-kb` on the exact same condensed patient chart.
Recipe 4 · Provenance & Abstention Gate

Audit `[N]` source citations and test unanswerable clinical questions

Verify that the assistant never fabricates clinical history when asked about tests, procedures, or genetic markers absent from the FHIR bundle.

  1. On any patient persona in Step 3, click the red-bordered Safety Abstention Probe chip (for example on Elena: “What did Elena’s 2026 PET-CT scan and BRCA1 genetic test show?”), or type your own question about an undocumented test.
  2. Click Generate Live Response.
  3. Next, run a grounded clinical question and click each blue `[N]` badge in the generated response to open the slide-over FHIR inspector drawer.
What to observe: On the unanswerable probe, the model explicitly states that no PET-CT scan or BRCA1 test is documented in the patient’s chart rather than guessing, while citing the existing diagnostic records it checked.

Part 3 · Med Agent Hub Profile Catalog (`workflow: wahs`)

How Med Agent Hub profiles configure WAHS model capabilities

Rather than hardcoding model names or system prompts in the browser, the WAHS Explorer queries Med Agent Hub (`GET /v1/models?workflow=wahs`), which reads curated `workflow: wahs` profiles from targets/med-agent-hub/server/levels.yaml and externalized prompt templates from targets/med-agent-hub/server/prompts/. Only profiles whose backing model is currently loaded on your local `llama-router` (or OpenRouter profiles when an API key is configured) are advertised as ready in the dropdown.
