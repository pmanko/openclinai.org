WA Health Summary (WAHS) · Interactive Tour & Manual Testing Guide

WAHS Patient Chart Explorer — Walkthrough & Experimentation Guide.

The WAHS Patient Chart Explorer is both an interactive demonstration of patient-mediated FHIR R4 / IPS chart condensation and a live experimentation workbench on wahs-demo.openclinai.org. Every choice you make in Step 1 (Patient & Raw FHIR Chart) feeds into Step 2 (Condense & Prepare Chart) across 7 token budgets (64k down to 1k), and the exact condensed chart you configure in Step 2 flows directly into Step 3 (Skills & Q&A) for live testing against our Cloud Med Agent Hub (backed by the shared UW CIRG GPU server), direct UW GPU Server models, local open-weight models, or OpenRouter.

Part 2 · Hands-On Experimentation Tutorial

Four reproducible recipes for manual testing & model comparison

Use these four step-by-step recipes on wahs-demo.openclinai.org (using either the default 🏥 Cloud Med Agent Hub / 🎓 UW GPU Server with Google Sign-In, or a local backend) to test token-budget sensitivity (`64k` down to `1k`), knowledge-base augmentation (`-kb` vs. `-raw`), and safety abstention.

Recipe 1 · Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3

Compare `64k` / `8k` vs. `1k` condensation on a multi-year longitudinal chart

Test how token-budget condensation affects prompt size, latency, and historical recall across our 5-year longitudinal US Core archetypes and 7,099-entry Synthea charts.

  1. In Step 1, click Maurice Auer (`7,099 items` · 18.8 MB) or Elena Rostova (`454 items` · 5-year Cardiorenal HFrEF).
  2. Go to Step 2, inspect the `ⓘ` tooltips on the levers, and compare `64k` / `16k` (Tier 1 full 5-year history retained) against `8k` and `1k`.
  3. Go to Step 3, keep Prepare for My Next Doctor Visit selected in the Line 1 dropdown, and click ⚡ Generate Live. Note the telemetry pill (`Input: ( lines)`).
  4. Return to Step 2, switch the budget to `1k` (`1,024` tok), return to Step 3 (notice the generation area resets cleanly to blank for the new lever tuple), and click ⚡ Generate Live again.
What to observe: At 1k, prefill latency (`TTFT`) drops sharply while preserving active diagnoses, active medications, and allergies—and expanding 🔧 Advanced View → Inspect Full Condensed Prompt shows line [2] Completeness Manifest listing the exact omitted historical resource counts by category.
Recipe 2 · Decoupled KB vs. Raw Toggle & `🕓 Run History`

Compare `🧠 + 4-Layer KB` vs. `📄 Raw Chart Only` on any model—and flip between runs

Evaluate how much the deterministic 4-Layer Clinical Knowledge Base helps the language model catch cross-clinic conflicts and discharge-note hold contradictions.

  1. Select Elena Rostova (`454 items` · Cardiorenal HFrEF) in Step 1 and `8k` in Step 2 (inspect the Context Window Gauge at the top of Step 2 to see the `24k` Best-Perf limit for 12B models).
  2. In Step 3, open the Line 1 Clinical Task Dropdown and choose ⚠️ Medication Conflict & Duplicate Check.
  3. Keep `🧠 + 4-Layer KB` active in the top bar (or Step 2 lever) and click ⚡ Generate Live. Expand 🔧 Advanced View at the bottom to inspect the Hub Profile and 4-Layer KB findings.
  4. Click `📄 Raw Chart Only` in the top bar (`include_kb_findings: false`) and click ⚡ Generate Live again—then use the `🕓 Run History` dropdown (`#gen-history-select`) to switch back and forth between Run #1 and Run #2 with full context restoration.
What to observe: Under 📄 Raw Chart Only, the model must discover the contradiction between the active outpatient MedicationRequest [8, 9] and the free-text hospital discharge summary [17] purely from the raw condensed lines. Under 🧠 + 4-Layer KB, Layer 2’s structured conflict finding is injected alongside the chart, ensuring consistent citation of both source records.
Recipe 3 · Cloud Hub vs. Direct UW GPU (`>12B` Models) vs. OpenRouter

Switch between Cloud Med Agent Hub, Direct UW GPU Server (13 Models up to `26B`), and OpenRouter in `⚙️`

Compare curated Med Agent Hub profiles running on Cloud Run against direct UW GPU models (including `Gemma 4 26B MoE`, `Qwen 3 14B`, and `Qwen 2.5 14B`) or hosted frontier models via OpenRouter.

  1. Click the ⚙️ button in the top navigation bar to open the Connection Settings — Choose Model Backend modal.
  2. Option 1 — 🏥 Cloud Med Agent Hub (Default on `wahs-demo.openclinai.org`): Click Sign in with Google with your whitelisted account to access curated WAHS profiles (`wahs-26b-a4b-kb`, `wahs-qwen3-14b-kb`, `wahs-qwen25-14b-kb`, `wahs-12b-kb`, `wahs-mistral-nemo-12b-kb`, `wahs-qwen35-9b-kb`, `wahs-llama31-8b-kb`, `wahs-granite33-8b-kb`, `wahs-e4b-kb`, `wahs-qwen3-4b-kb`, `wahs-e2b-kb`) backed by the shared UW CIRG GPU server.
  3. Option 2 — 🎓 UW GPU Server (Direct): Click the 🎓 UW GPU Server card to query all 13 Ollama models on the UW GPU server (`ollama/gemma4:26b`, `ollama/qwen3:14b`, `ollama/qwen2.5:14b`, `ollama/mistral-nemo:12b`, `ollama/gemma4:12b/e4b/e2b`, `ollama/qwen3:8b/4b`, `ollama/llama3.1:8b`, `ollama/granite3.3:8b`, `ollama/mistral:7b`, `ollama/smollm2:latest`).
  4. Option 3 — ☁️ OpenRouter: Click ☁️ OpenRouter, paste your sk-or-v1-... key, and click 🔌 Test Connection or Apply.
What to observe: Your Google Sign-In token is shared seamlessly across both 🏥 Cloud Med Agent Hub and 🎓 UW GPU Server during your browser session—switch between them in one click without signing in twice.
Recipe 4 · Provenance & Abstention Gate

Audit `[N]` source citations and test unanswerable clinical questions

Verify that the assistant never fabricates clinical history when asked about tests, procedures, or allergies absent from the FHIR bundle.

  1. In Step 3, click into the Line 2 Custom Question Box (`#qa-question-input`) to open the suggestions popover before typing, then type colonoscopy (or select the abstention probe question) and click ⚡ Generate Live.
  2. Next, run a grounded clinical task and click any blue `[N]` badge in the generated summary or Grounded FHIR Sources bar to open the slide-over FHIR inspector drawer.
What to observe: On the unanswerable probe, the model begins with an explicit safety abstention (`ABSTAIN:`) confirming it checked the patient’s chart and found no record of the requested procedure rather than guessing.

Part 3 · Cloud GPU Sign-In & Running Local Backends

How to connect via Cloud Med Agent Hub / UW GPU (zero local setup) or boot your own local stack

The spacious ⚙️ Connection Settings modal supports seven one-click backend presets: two cloud presets backed by the shared UW CIRG GPU server (🏥 Cloud Med Agent Hub and 🎓 UW GPU Server, enabled by default on wahs-demo.openclinai.org), four local workstation presets (🏠 Local Med Agent Hub, 🖥️ LM Studio, ⚡ llama-router, and 🦙 Ollama), and ☁️ OpenRouter.

Path 0 · Default Cloud Stack (Zero Local Setup)

Sign in to 🏥 Cloud Med Agent Hub or 🎓 UW GPU Server on `wahs-demo.openclinai.org`

Run live WAHS profiles and open-weight GPU models directly in your browser without installing Docker, LM Studio, or local GGUF weights.

  1. Open https://wahs-demo.openclinai.org/. By default, 🏥 Cloud Med Agent Hub is selected (`🔒 Cloud Hub (Sign In ⚙️)`).
  2. Click ⚙️ in the top bar and click Sign in with Google using your whitelisted `@uw.edu` or researcher Google account.
  3. Once signed in, the status banner turns green (🟢 Cloud Hub (UW GPU • your-email@uw.edu)) and populates the top-bar dropdown with curated `-kb` and `-raw` WAHS profiles routed through our Cloud Run `med-agent-hub` service to the UW CIRG GPU server.
  4. To test raw GPU models without the Hub layer, open ⚙️ and click 🎓 UW GPU Server—your active Google session connects immediately.
Architecture note: Browser requests authenticate through wahs-gpu-auth-relay on Google Cloud Run, which verifies your whitelisted Google identity before forwarding to wahs-cloud-med-agent-hub and https://llm.co.dev.cirg.uw.edu.
Path A · Local Full Stack (`:18081` + `:8077`)

Boot Local Med Agent Hub + `llama-router` (36 Simple Profiles across 7 Families)

Runs the full OpenClinAI local stack on your workstation with curated `-kb` and `-raw` single-model profiles and automatic on-demand GGUF provisioning.

  1. From the openclinai.org repository root, start the local GGUF model router (auto-verifies and downloads pinned models into ~/.cache/llama-router-models):
    ./scripts/llama-router-up.sh
  2. Start med-agent-hub on port 18081:
    docker compose -f compose/openmrs-2.8-refapp.yml up -d med-agent-hub
  3. Verify that all 36 local WAHS profiles are advertised:
    curl -sS "http://localhost:18081/v1/models?workflow=wahs" | jq '.data | length'
  4. Open http://localhost:18081/wahs/demo (same-origin) or click ⚙️ → 🏠 Local Med Agent Hub (:18081) on https://wahs-demo.openclinai.org/.
Zero-friction model provisioning: Even if a pinned model in levels.yaml is not yet in ~/.cache/llama-router-models, med-agent-hub automatically streams and SHA-256 verifies its GGUF on first request.
Path B · LM Studio Bionic (`:1235`) & Classic (`:1234`)

Connect LM Studio Bionic or Classic (GGUF / Apple Silicon MLX) to the WAHS Explorer

Use LM Studio Bionic (:1235) or LM Studio Classic (:1234) as a standalone local OpenAI-compatible server on macOS, Windows, or Linux.

  1. Zero-copy import of existing OpenClinAI GGUFs (optional): If you already have GGUF models in ~/.cache/llama-router-models, hard-link them into LM Studio with zero extra disk usage (note -L hard link, as LM Studio’s scanner skips -l symlinks):
    ~/.lmstudio/bin/lms import -y -L --user-repo unsloth/gemma-4-12B-it-GGUF ~/.cache/llama-router-models/gemma-4-12b.gguf
  2. Start the LM Studio local server with CORS enabled (port 1235 on Bionic, or 1234 on Classic):
    ~/.lmstudio/bin/lms server start --port 1235 --cors
  3. Set default Context Length to at least 16384 in LM Studio settings so `8k` and `16k` condensed patient charts fit without truncation.
  4. In the WAHS Explorer top bar, click ⚙️ → 🖥️ LM Studio (:1235 / :1234). All local models appear grouped by family in the dropdown with paired `+ 4-Layer Clinical KB` and `Raw Condensed Chart Only` modes.
Automatic reasoning-budget handling: The WAHS Explorer automatically passes reasoning_effort: "none" and chat_template_kwargs: {"enable_thinking": false} so clinical responses stream immediately into the summary pane.
Path C · Direct Raw Model Server (`:8077` / `:8076` / `:11434`) & Browser Blockers

Connect directly to `llama-router` or Ollama—and resolve HTTPS → localhost browser prompts

Point the browser directly at a local OpenAI-compatible server without Docker, or troubleshoot browser Local Network Access permissions when calling localhost from https://wahs-demo.openclinai.org.

  1. For `llama.cpp` (`llama-router`): Run ./scripts/llama-router-up.sh (listens on :8077 or :8076) and click ⚙️ → ⚡ llama-router (:8077 / :8076).
  2. For Ollama (`:11434`): Run OLLAMA_ORIGINS="*" OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH=16384 ollama serve and click ⚙️ → 🦙 Ollama (:11434).
  3. Chrome Local Network Access & Brave/Safari tips: When connecting from https://wahs-demo.openclinai.org to a localhost port, allow Local network access in Chrome’s address-bar site settings (or open http://localhost:18081/wahs/demo directly for same-origin access).
Need zero localhost configuration? Use Path 0 (🏥 Cloud Med Agent Hub / 🎓 UW GPU Server) above—it runs entirely over HTTPS on Google Cloud Run and requires zero local ports or browser permissions.

Part 4 · Med Agent Hub Profile Catalog (`workflow: wahs`)

How Med Agent Hub single-model profiles configure WAHS across 7 model families

Rather than hardcoding model names or system prompts in the browser, the WAHS Explorer queries Med Agent Hub (`GET /v1/models?workflow=wahs`), which reads curated single-model (`topology: single`) `workflow: wahs` profiles from targets/med-agent-hub/server/levels.yaml and externalized prompt templates from targets/med-agent-hub/server/prompts/. Every model family provides paired `-kb` (KB-Guided) and `-raw` (Raw Unhinted Control) profiles.

Part 5 · Deeper Reading & Published Evidence

Explore the WAHS evaluation reports and technical architecture

Interactive app & overview

  • Launch the WAHS Patient Chart Explorer (wahs-demo.openclinai.org) → Open the live 3-step application to run the recipes on this page.
  • WA Health Summary project overview → Standards foundation (HL7 IPS, US Core, KDIGO, RxNorm, 42 CFR Part 2, WA MHMDA) and workstream summary.
  • Med Agent Hub & WAHS+ clinical graph architecture → Technical specification of the patient clinical entity graph, conditional Tier-0 overflow recovery, and bounded pre-generation KB orchestration.
  • Detailed design & architecture dashboard → In-depth technical synthesis covering transfer opportunities, FHIR AI-Provenance, and Phase 3 roadmap.

Published evaluation reports

  • 7-Family simple profile benchmark & blinded dual-judge evaluation (W20) → 112-cell factorial evaluation (`8 cases × 14 simple profiles`) across Gemma 4, MedGemma, Qwen, Llama, Mistral, Granite, and LFM2 (`-kb` vs. `-raw`), scored by blinded Claude Opus 5.5 + Gemini 4 Argon (`α = 0.90–0.96`).
  • Live local model screening & KB agent team evaluation (W8 & W13) → 3-stage local model funnel (1.5B–35B GGUF) and 4-arm comparison over unhinted `lossless_structural` charts (`83.5 → 85.5 → 88.5 → 93.0 / 100`).
  • Condensation budget ablation (`16k`–`1k`) & overflow chart retrieval (W7 & W13) → Token-budget ablation across 14 FHIR R4 bundles showing where static `2k`/`1k` condensation drops older history and how `W7` read-only chart lookup tools recover recall (`75.1 → 92.6 / 100`).
