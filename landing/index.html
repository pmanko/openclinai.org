University of Washington · Digital Initiatives Group at I-TECH (DIGI)

Grounded clinical AI applications, a shared context engine, and verifiable evidence

OpenClinAI develops open-source clinical question-answering and population-reporting tools alongside the context condensation, deterministic verification, and post-training infrastructure required to make small open-weight models reliable in real health systems.

Rather than treating clinical AI as a single prompt wrapper, the platform separates point-of-care and analytics client applications, a shared clinical AI engine (Med Agent Hub), a 4-layer evaluation harness, and an empirical post-training laboratory.

02 · Clinical AI Engine & Context Architecture

Med Agent Hub: shared clinical context, deterministic rules, and checked answers

Small and mid-sized open-weight models fail when handed raw, noisy EHR exports or unverified single-shot prompts. Med Agent Hub sits behind client applications to normalize clinical context, surface guideline rules before generation, and verify every claim against source records.

Layer A · Context Ingestion

QueryStore & 5-Tier Lossless Condenser

Caches normalized OpenMRS patient snapshots in QueryStore and condenses multi-clinic FHIR R4 bundles across 9 token budgets (256k down to 1k) with an explicit [Completeness Manifest] and conditional Tier-0 raw note/lab overflow recovery.

Layer B · Pre-Generation Intelligence

Clinical Entity Graph & 4-Layer KB

Deterministically links medications, conditions, renal/lab safety thresholds, and note-vs-structured contradictions (ClinicalEntityGraph) while injecting scoped clinical reference rules from WHO SMART Guidelines, KDIGO, ADA, CDC ACIP, RxNorm, and LOINC.

Layer C · Staged Answer Lifecycle

Answer Rules (R1–R10) & Verification

Streams SSE stages (Context → Answer → Verification → In-Depth) governed by 10 deterministic clinical answer rules (temporal window anchoring, chronological lab trajectories, pregnancy/pediatric guardrails) and findings-not-grades citation checks.

Layer D · Model Routing

Local-First & Cloud GPU Profiles

Exposes stream-isolated model profiles (openmrs-*, catalyst-sql-*, wahs-*, lab-laya-*) backed by local Apple Silicon / Linux engines (llama-router, MLX, vLLM) or authenticated Cloud GPU relays across Gemma 4, MedGemma, and Qwen3.

03 · Evaluation Approaches & Verification

Validation Harness & The 4-Layer Clinical Verification Stack

Generic LLM-as-a-judge averages hide rare, high-severity clinical omissions and fabricated citations. The Validation Harness evaluates clinical AI systems across a 4-layer verification hierarchy, interactive streaming telemetry, and 34 published evaluation reports.

Layer 1 · Deterministic Oracles

Provenance, Temporal, SQL & CQL

Zero-variance programmatic checks run on every output: exact FHIR/OpenMRS UUID citation binding, temporal window gates, PostgreSQL row-set equivalence (_compare_row_sets), and deterministic WHO SMART L3 CQL execution (cqf-ruler).

Layer 2 · System-1 Encoders

Pinned Local Entailment Verifiers

Fast, reproducible single-pass cross-encoders (Laya ~0.4B) verify claim-to-record entailment (supported / partly / unsupported) and appropriate abstention in under 50 ms without generative API drift.

Layer 3 · Multi-Family Autoraters

17-Dimension Rubric & Safety Floor

Blinded cross-family LLM judge panels (Claude Opus, GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro) score a 3-Domain × 17-Dimension clinical taxonomy that separates Comprehensiveness from Critical Safety Omissions (Safety-Adjusted Benchmark) and measures perturbation F1_floor.

Layer 4 · Clinician Calibration

Blind Adjudication & PPI Intervals

Practising clinicians perform dual blind review on stratified subsets, paired with Prediction-Powered Inference (ppi_benchmark) to rectify LLM autorater bias, compute valid 95% confidence intervals, and score Script Concordance (SCT) under uncertainty.

04 · Post-Training & Decision-Layer Lab

Non-autoregressive clinical verifiers and the 5-rung post-training ladder

Our AI engineering lab investigates how far sub-billion System-1 encoders and compact 1.7B–4B open-weight generators can be pushed on edge and single-GPU clinic hardware when trained against deterministic clinical and execution oracles.

Track A · System-1 Decision Layer

Laya (~0.4B) Non-Autoregressive Clinical Classifier & Verifier

Calling a second generative LLM pass just to check citations or filter chart records consumes 25% of turn latency (1.5–4.0s). We evaluate compact single-forward-pass encoders (Laya ~0.4B vs. Jev-1.13 reference) across two clinical gating tasks:

  • Phase 0a — Context-Admission Relevance: 5-fold Leave-One-Patient-Out (LOPO) evaluation across 7,840 clinical record–topic pairs under a recall-first safety gate (≥99% must-include record recall).
  • Phase 0b — Claim Grounding & Review-Error Gating: Sub-50ms single-pass Choice / Boolean / Score verification for claim entailment and deterministic rule compliance.
  • Logit Temperature Calibration: Post-hoc temperature scaling (T = 3.297) reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 0.466 to 0.081 without changing ranking AUROC.
Track B · Generative Post-Training

The 5-Rung Post-Training Ladder for Edge Clinical Models

A controlled experimental progression testing where inference-time compute stops and weight adaptation begins for 1.7B–4B clinical models (Gemma 4 E2B/E4B, Qwen3 1.7B/4B):

  • Rung 1 — Constrained Decoding & Best-of-N: Grammar-constrained citation/JSON emission paired with Layer 1 + Laya verifier reranking.
  • Rung 2 — Parameter-Efficient SFT ("LoRA Without Regret"): Rank and all-linear module sweeps on citation-grounded clinical Q&A and FHIR/CQL synthesis.
  • Rung 3 — On-Policy Distillation: Distilling 12B/27B teacher trajectories into 1.7B–4B students on student-generated prefixes.
  • Rung 4 — RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR / GRPO): Reinforcement learning driven by PostgreSQL row-set equivalence and WHO SMART CQL execution oracles.
  • Rung 5 — Quantization & Tokenizer Fertility: BF16 → Q8_0 → Q4_K_M safety curves, LMIC multilingual token fertility, and roofline bandwidth profiling.

About OpenClinAI

Built for real clinical workflows and transparent evidence

OpenClinAI is developed by the Digital Initiatives Group at I-TECH (DIGI) at the University of Washington. We focus on open-source health systems used across global health and public-sector care delivery, including OpenMRS, OpenELIS Global, Apache Superset, and FHIR R4 exchanges.

Demonstration sites, recordings, and benchmark reports use synthetic or de-identified evaluation data. They are research and engineering evidence, not clinical guidance or certification of clinical safety.
