Condensation-Tier Information-Loss Ablation, Hybrid Full-Chart Queryability & Messy Multi-EHR Cohort Evaluation

This report answers two core Phase 3 engineering questions: (1) How much clinical detail is lost as we condense a multi-megabyte patient chart down from 16,384 tokens (Tier 1) to 2,048 tokens (Tier 4), and can on-demand full-chart lookup tools recover deep historical facts without blowing up the prompt context? and (2) How does our 4-Layer clinical lookup perform when 25%–60% of incoming EHR entries lack standard SNOMED/RxNorm/LOINC codes or contain free-text clinical notes that contradict active medication lists?

01 · Horizon-Stratified Condensation & Hybrid Retrieval Ablation (160 Evaluation Cells)

We evaluated five condensation and retrieval configurations across all 32 WAHS clinical evaluation cases, stratified by Question Horizon: Current Active State (active meds, allergies, active conditions, privacy redaction), 24-Month Trend (eGFR, HbA1c, immunization series), Full-Text Note / Perioperative Detail (e.g., November 2023 CABG perioperative Cefazolin 2000 MG Injection), and Deep Historical / Abstention Probes.

Key Finding: Under non-destructive lossless_structural condensation without pre-baked conflict hints (W12 / W13), generous budgets (16,384t and 8,192t) score 88.5 / 100, while tighter static windows degrade monotonically to 83.1 / 100 at 4,096t and 70.4 / 100 at 2,048t as historical immunizations, resolved conditions, and 2023 perioperative records fall outside the static window. Enabling the read-only overflow lookup tools (search_chart_entries, get_lab_history, read_clinical_note in wahs-tier4-2k-hybrid-tools) recovers +18.2 points (to 88.6 / 100) while keeping the base prompt inside 2,048 tokens and maintaining 0 privacy leaks in opt-in external_share mode.

02 · Messy Synthetic Cohort (Set C) & De-Identified Real Clinical Summaries (Pathway A + Pathway B)

To avoid circularity from evaluating only clean Synthea codes, we evaluated both Pathway A (Set C — Pragmatic Messy Multi-Source), which strips standard SNOMED/RxNorm/CVX/LOINC codes from 25% of clinical entries and injects free-text clinical note hold/stop instructions that contradict active MedicationRequest resources, and Pathway B (De-Identified Real Clinical Summaries) modeled on MIMIC-IV cardiorenal discharge summaries and SMART IPS transition records (where 40%–60% of entries carry free-text descriptions only).
