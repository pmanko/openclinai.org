01 · Executive Abstract: What We Are Evaluating & Why It Matters

WA Health Summary (WAHS) is a patient-mediated clinical summary application developed by the University of Washington Clinical Informatics Research Group (CIRG) and the Washington State Department of Health. Using SMART Health Links and patient-authorized TEFCA / patient-portal FHIR R4 connections, individuals can pull their clinical records from multiple hospitals, primary care clinics, and pharmacies into a single personal health summary based on the HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) standard.

When patients aggregate multi-decade records from multiple health systems and attempt to use AI to summarize their chart or prepare for an appointment, four critical failure modes appear in standard LLM pipelines:

1. Context Window Overflow & EHR Bloat

A complex chronic-disease patient chart (Maurice742_Auer97, 84 years of care) exports as an 18.36 MB FHIR R4 bundle containing 7,660 resources and ~3.52 million raw tokens — exceeding standard 128k–200k LLM context windows by 17× to 27×. Nearly 97% of observation entries are repetitive lab values, vital-sign flowsheets, claims noise, and duplicate Base64-encoded notes.

2. Silent Cross-Clinic Contradictions

When Clinic A and Regional Medical Center B both export records for the same patient, dangerous discrepancies arise: duplicate high-intensity statins prescribed concurrently, a diuretic stopped at one clinic after hypotension/AKI but still listed active at another, or unrecorded CKD progression across serial eGFR labs.

3. Unaudited Privacy Leakage on External Share

Patients frequently share a health summary with an external specialist, caregiver, or community provider. Raw FHIR exports contain sensitive Social Determinants of Health (SDOH), intimate partner violence (IPV), behavioral health, and reproductive/substance-use records governed by 42 CFR Part 2 and the WA My Health My Data Act (MHMDA).

4. Missing Resource Provenance & Hallucinations

Prompting a frontier LLM directly over truncated FHIR JSON produces fluent narrative prose with zero verifiable links back to source FHIR resources, and models frequently guess when asked about unrecorded facts (such as blood type, advance directives, or genetic panels).

03 · Architecture Under Test & Deterministic Scoring Gates

Rather than passing raw FHIR JSON directly to a language model, our architecture separates deterministic clinical condensation and rule verification (in med-agent-hub/server/fhir/) from automated evaluation and provenance auditing (in validation-harness/harness/wahs/):

5-Tier Progressive FHIR R4 / IPS Condenser

  • Tier 0 (Raw Baseline): Unmodified FHIR R4 bundle (up to 3.52M tokens).
  • Tier 1 (Structural Pruning & Note Hashing): Strips FHIR boilerplate, narrative HTML, and claims noise; deduplicates identical Base64 clinical notes by SHA-256 hash (791 → 8 unique notes in Maurice’s chart).
  • Tier 2 (Clinical Consolidation): Groups active vs. historical medications, resolves problem-list status, and collapses 3,760 raw observations into 116 longitudinal LOINC chains (Latest + Min/Max + 3-point trajectory).
  • Tier 3 (Budget-Targeted Rolling Window): Restricts routine encounters/labs to a rolling 24-month window while preserving lifetime high-acuity surgeries (e.g., CABG) and active chronic conditions.
  • Tier 4 (Emergency IPS Safety Core): Guarantees a ≤1,024-token International Patient Summary containing 100% of active allergies, active medications, active conditions, and high-severity clinical conflict alerts.

4-Layer Deterministic Clinical Knowledge Base

  • Layer 1 (SNOMED CT Disease Progression): Maps 108 condition concepts across 18 disease families; cross-checks lab trajectories (KDIGO eGFR < 30 Stage 4 CKD; ADA HbA1c ≥ 6.5%) against coded problem lists.
  • Layer 2 (RxNorm Therapeutic Classes & Safety): Maps 94 medication codes across 14 classes; detects duplicate therapy (dual statins, dual antihistamines), stale acute orders, and missing anaphylaxis epinephrine rescue.
  • Layer 3 (CDC ACIP CVX & LOINC Chains): Maps 48 vaccine codes and 280+ LOINC codes; validates ACIP minimum dose intervals and collapses same-day duplicate lab panels.
  • Layer 4 (42 CFR Part 2 & WA MHMDA Privacy Gate): Enforces posture-aware redaction before condensation when sharing_posture="external_share".

Five Deterministic Evaluation Gates in harness.wahs

Every evaluation case in wahs_eval_cases.jsonl is scored across five deterministic gates:

  1. Token Budget Gate (budget_met): Verifies that the condensed clinical ledger satisfies actual_tokens ≤ target_tokens at every tested budget (8,192, 4,096, and 2,048 tokens).
  2. Target Resource UUID Recall (target_uuid_recall): Verifies that the exact ground-truth FHIR resourceUuid entries for the clinical anomaly, condition, or encounter are retained and cited.
  3. Citation Provenance Resolution (citation_resolution_rate): Audits every [N] bracket citation in the generated output against the condenser’s citation_map and flags any unresolved or fabricated citation.
  4. External-Share Privacy Gate (privacy_leaked == False): Scans both the condensed chart text and the final answer for any forbidden sensitive SDOH / IPV / 42 CFR Part 2 terms or UUIDs when sharing_posture="external_share".
  5. Unanswerable Abstention Gate (abstention_correct): Verifies that prompts asking for unrecorded clinical facts explicitly abstain rather than confabulating values.
