01 · Executive Abstract: What We Are Evaluating & Why It Matters

WA Health Summary (WAHS) is a patient-mediated clinical summary application developed by the University of Washington Clinical Informatics Research Group (CIRG) and the Washington State Department of Health. Using SMART Health Links and patient-authorized TEFCA / patient-portal FHIR R4 connections, individuals can pull their clinical records from multiple hospitals, primary care clinics, and pharmacies into a single personal health summary based on the HL7 International Patient Summary (IPS) standard.

When patients aggregate multi-decade records from multiple health systems and attempt to use AI to summarize their chart or prepare for an appointment, four critical failure modes appear in standard LLM pipelines:

1. Context Window Overflow & EHR Bloat

A complex chronic-disease patient chart (Maurice742_Auer97, 84 years of care) exports as an 18.36 MB FHIR R4 bundle containing 7,660 resources and ~3.52 million raw tokens — exceeding standard 128k–200k LLM context windows by 17× to 27×. Nearly 97% of observation entries are repetitive lab values, vital-sign flowsheets, claims noise, and duplicate Base64-encoded notes.

2. Silent Cross-Clinic Contradictions

When Clinic A and Regional Medical Center B both export records for the same patient, dangerous discrepancies arise: duplicate high-intensity statins prescribed concurrently, a diuretic stopped at one clinic after hypotension/AKI but still listed active at another, or unrecorded CKD progression across serial eGFR labs.

3. Unaudited Privacy Leakage on External Share

Patients frequently share a health summary with an external specialist, caregiver, or community provider. Raw FHIR exports contain sensitive Social Determinants of Health (SDOH), intimate partner violence (IPV), behavioral health, and reproductive/substance-use records governed by 42 CFR Part 2 and the WA My Health My Data Act (MHMDA).

4. Missing Resource Provenance & Hallucinations

Prompting a frontier LLM directly over truncated FHIR JSON produces fluent narrative prose with zero verifiable links back to source FHIR resources, and models frequently guess when asked about unrecorded facts (such as blood type, advance directives, or genetic panels).

03 · Plain-English Guide to Our Architecture, Metrics & Scoring Gates

Rather than passing raw FHIR JSON directly to a language model, our Phase 2 architecture separates deterministic clinical chart condensation and rule-based safety checks (in med-agent-hub/server/fhir/) from automated evaluation and source-record verification (in validation-harness/harness/wahs/). Because technical labels like “4-Layer KB Code Coverage” or “Multi-Source Anomaly Recall” can read like opaque buzzwords, the table below translates every term used in this report into plain language, shows how it is calculated, and gives a concrete clinical example.

How the 5-Tier Condenser Shrinks a Chart Step by Step

  • Tier 0 (Full Normalized Ledger): Strips repetitive FHIR JSON syntax, HTML blocks, and billing/claims noise while keeping all clinical entries.
  • Tier 1 (Note Deduplication & Panel Grouping): Removes duplicate Base64-encoded clinical notes using SHA-256 content hashes (collapsing 791 → 8 unique notes in Maurice’s chart) and groups same-day metabolic lab panels.
  • Tier 2 (Longitudinal Clinical Consolidation): Separates active from stopped medications and folds 3,760 raw lab readings into 116 longitudinal trend lines (showing First, Latest, Min/Max, and Recent Trend).
  • Tier 3 (24-Month Rolling Window + Major Surgeries): Limits routine office visits and routine labs to the last 24 months while keeping major lifetime surgeries (such as CABG heart surgery) and all active chronic conditions.
  • Tier 4 (Emergency Safety Core ≤ 1,024–2,048 Tokens): Retains 100% of active allergies, active medications, active conditions, implanted devices, and high-priority cross-clinic conflict alerts inside a compact summary.

How the 4-Layer Clinical Rule Engine Checks the Chart

  • Layer 1 — Condition & Disease Stage Rules (SNOMED CT): Checks whether mutually exclusive disease stages (such as CKD Stages 1, 2, and 3) are all marked “active” at the same time, and whether recent kidney (eGFR) or diabetes (HbA1c) labs show progression not yet updated on the problem list.
  • Layer 2 — Medication Safety & Ingredient Rules (RxNorm): Maps brand names (Plavix, Lipitor, Prinivil) to generic ingredients (Clopidogrel, Atorvastatin, Lisinopril) to catch duplicate prescriptions, dose discrepancies across clinics, and missing emergency rescue medications (such as missing epinephrine for peanut anaphylaxis).
  • Layer 3 — Vaccine Schedules & Lab Harmonization (CDC ACIP & LOINC): Flags duplicate same-day immunizations across clinic and state registry exports, checks minimum intervals between vaccine doses, and merges equivalent blood vs. serum lab codes.
  • Layer 4 — Pre-Condensation Privacy Filter (42 CFR Part 2 & WA MHMDA): Strips sensitive domestic-violence, housing-instability, and behavioral screening entries before condensation whenever the user selects external_share.

09 · Candid Methodological Audit, Current Gaps & Phase 3 Live-LLM Evaluation Plan

To ensure scientific rigor and avoid over-claiming from near-100% prototype metrics, we audited the Phase 2 evaluation pipeline, synthetic dataset construction, and scoring harness against current clinical AI evaluation standards (including OpenAI HealthBench, Stanford MedHELM, VeriFact / FActScore, and our own blind multi-judge evaluation protocol in specs/judging.md). Readers and collaborators should interpret the Phase 2 results above with five explicit caveats:

1. Deterministic Rule Verification vs. Live Generative LLM Evaluation

In Phase 2, the condenser (condenser.py) and clinical analyzer/responder (wahs_analyzer.py) execute deterministic Python normalization and structured response templates rather than live generative completions from open-weight language models (such as Gemma 4 12B, MedGemma, or Qwen 3). Thus, the 99.4 / 100 score proves that our deterministic pre-LLM condensation and clinical rule checks accurately extract and link the 32 target facts—not that an open-weight LLM reading that condensed ledger achieves 99.4% generative accuracy. Conversely, the 61.2 / 100 Opus 5.5 row is a reconstructed reference baseline from Phase 1 observations over manually sliced JSON rather than a live head-to-head run over all 32 prompts.

2. Co-Designed Perturbations & Lookup Tables (Ceiling Effect)

The 15 cross-clinic conflicts in Set B (perturbations.py) and the 4-Layer reference tables (rxnorm_lookup.json, snomed_families.json, acip_rules.json) were authored in the same development cycle over the exact codes present in the three test charts. Because the anomaly generator and rule detector share the same finite vocabulary, code recognition and conflict recall reach 100.0% by construction. This validates software rule correctness (integration verification), not out-of-distribution recall on unseen hospital coding variations.

3. Small Cohort (N = 3) & Clean Synthea Regularity

All three charts originate from Synthea, where every diagnosis carries a clean SNOMED CT code, every drug carries a clean RxNorm code, and every lab carries a standard LOINC code. Real-world patient-mediated exports (and Epic EHI tables) contain uncoded free-text problem lists, local hospital charge codes, scanned PDF/OCR discharge summaries, and garbled sig instructions where deterministic lookup coverage alone will drop substantially.

4. Harness vs. In-Process Scorer Schema Audit

While our in-process evaluation script (generate_wahs_eval_report.py) scored 32 / 32 (100%), our initial standalone HTTP harness run (summary.json, recording 81 / 96 cells across 3 budgets) surfaced two scorer bugs: (a) a JSON key mismatch between abstained (emitted by wahs_analyzer.py) and abstains (checked by harness.wahs.evaluator), and (b) a substring false-positive where the system’s own transparency banner (“Sensitive SDOH items were redacted”) triggered the forbidden-term regex. Documenting and fixing these schema boundaries prevents silent scorer drift in Phase 3.
