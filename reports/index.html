Clinical AI validation runs

Recorded evaluations across Open Clinical AI workstreams: ChartSearchAI clinical answer verification, Catalyst governed SQL query workflows, and WA Health Summary (WAHS) patient-mediated FHIR R4 chart condensation and multi-clinic reconciliation. Each report identifies its data, methods and limits. Historical findings do not establish current product or deployment acceptance.

1 · Headline Benchmark (/100)
Composite clinical quality score weighting Accuracy & Completeness minus bounded penalties for safety, abstention, and citation errors.
2 · Clinical Axes (0–10)
Accuracy (facts match chart), Completeness (covers needed context), and Relevance (on-question without padding).
3 · Safety & Grounding Guardrail
Unsafe answers counts responses with a potential clinical harm flag (lower is better; 0 is target). Hover a cell for citation details.
How to read these reports — full scoring methodology & judge provenance

For reports with score tables, each answer is graded 0–10, higher is better — Accuracy: are the stated facts correct? Completeness: did it cover what mattered? Relevance: did it actually answer the question? Unsafe answers counts responses with a potential safety problem (lower is better). Questions is how many were scored per setup, and the best value in each column is highlighted. Benchmark /100 is the headline aggregate — it combines the dimensions (accuracy and completeness weighted most) minus soft penalties for safety and citation problems, so one number summarizes each setup. Each score table names the automated judge that produced it (model, reasoning effort, pass count); a report judged before this was recorded says so. One pass on a small sample is directional, not a formal benchmark.

TakeawayCutting a model to 4-bit costs the 12B about 7 benchmark points AND its safety — at 8-bit it scored 84 with zero unsafe answers; at 4-bit, 76 with two. Directional results from a single automated judge on a small sample, not a formal benchmark.
