Clinical AI validation runs

How well do open-weight AI models read complex medical charts, catch medication conflicts across clinics, and answer clinical questions safely? Each report below tests different AI setups on realistic patient records — showing where they succeed, where they miss important facts, and why.

1 · Headline Benchmark (/100)
Composite clinical quality score weighting Accuracy & Completeness minus bounded penalties for safety, abstention, and citation errors.
2 · Clinical Axes (0–10)
Accuracy (facts match chart), Completeness (covers needed context), and Relevance (on-question without padding).
3 · Safety & Grounding Guardrail
Unsafe answers counts responses with a potential clinical harm flag (lower is better; 0 is target). Hover a cell for citation details.
How to read these reports — full scoring methodology & judge provenance

For reports with score tables, each answer is graded 0–10 (higher is better) — Accuracy: are the stated facts true to the chart? Completeness: did it cover the clinically important details? Relevance: did it directly answer the question in clear language? Unsafe answers counts responses with a potential safety or privacy problem (lower is better). Benchmark /100 is the overall headline score combining accuracy, completeness, and relevance minus penalties for safety or citation errors.

TakeawayFeeding a bigger chart to an AI backfires if the server's active memory window is smaller than the prompt. Scores peaked at the standard 8k summary, which fit comfortably inside the server's 16k memory limit: Gemma 4 26B with clinical rules led at 88.0/100 (8.75 accuracy, 9.12 completeness, zero unsafe answers), followed by Gemma 4 12B with clinical rules (80.5/100) and chart-only (80.2/100), while Qwen 3 14B scored 67.0/100. On the tight 1k summary, Med Agent Hub's clinical rule safety net beat chart-only by +19 points (43.8 vs. 24.8/100). When we sent 32k to 128k of chart text to that same 16k server, the server silently cut off the beginning of the prompt — erasing active allergies, current medications, and recent hospital stays — so chart-only scores collapsed to 0.0–2.8/100 (with 4/4 unsafe answers). Because Med Agent Hub attaches its pre-computed clinical conflict alerts at the very end of the prompt, those alerts survived the cutoff and rescued the medication conflict portion of the answer (27.0–32.8/100), though top-of-chart allergy checks remained cut off.
